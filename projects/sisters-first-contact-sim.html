The first 45 minutes with the nine and the twelve — mapped not as a scale from smooth to chaotic, but as the set of directions each moment can open into. Every beat has an opening, a divergence, a failure-state and an undercurrent. None of them is the bad one. The side quest — the recording conversation, if the moment is unhurried

The transcription pipeline is built, tested and waiting on exactly one thing: consent that was genuinely free to refuse. Monday is the first chance — but only if their mother has time to sit, and only after a session that went well enough that the ask isn't leaning on anything.

  • Say what it's for: you record so you can hear afterwards what you missed while teaching, and so the words each girl owns get written down accurately rather than from memory.
  • Say where it goes: your computer at home, never online, never anywhere else, deleted once the words are extracted. No account, no cloud, no company.
  • Make "no" free — and prove it: "if it's not comfortable, say no and nothing changes. The lessons are the same either way." Then stop talking. If the answer is anything other than a clear, easy yes, treat it as no and never raise it again.
  • Ask the girls too, separately. They're 9 and 12; being recorded is theirs to refuse independent of their mother, and asking them is also the most respectful thing they'll experience all week.
The power dynamic, stated plainlyTheir mother is employed by your mother, and you are teaching her children for free. Every one of those facts makes "no" more expensive for her to say. That is precisely why the offer must be made in a way that costs nothing to decline — and why a hesitant yes is not a yes. If in doubt: don't record.
