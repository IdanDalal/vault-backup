A3 — chunks: something has to be spoken to

Track A · Rung 3 · clean pass: ≥4 of 5 exchanges land

The rung needs an interlocutor who isn't you-the-adult-assessing, because answering a real person's real question in a foreign language is the thing that's frightening. The puppet was one solution and it's the wrong one for a nine-and-a-twelve who film themselves copying dances. Four alternatives, in order of how much they'd suit these two:

1 · You play the characterdefault — costs nothing, always available

You are a robot, a tourist who's lost, a very stupid king who only knows five sentences. "Hello! What's your name? … How are you? … I like pizza. What do you like?" — asked in character, badly, on a loop. A grown man committing to a ridiculous bit is unembarrassing in a way a puppet isn't; a twelve-year-old can enjoy it ironically without having to be a child about it, and a nine-year-old can enjoy it straight. Needs zero props and survives any energy level.

2 · The dubupgrade — if their world is on your phone

Fifteen muted seconds of something from Beat 3/4, and they supply the English voices. This is built directly on a thing you've watched them do voluntarily in this room — copy a screen until they have it. Highest ceiling of the four: prosody, chunk production, willingness to sound English, all at once. Dies instantly if you don't have a clip from a world they care about, so this one is prepared Sunday night or not at all.

3 · The whiteboard characterfallback — lowest energy cost

Draw a face on the board. It has a name and a bad voice, it asks the questions, and answers get given to it, not to you. Uses a surface that's already in play, uses drawing — a confirmed shared activity — and either girl can take the marker and become the character, which is the A2/A3 role-flip arriving for free.

4 · The voice messagenative medium — handle with the recording rule

Record five seconds of English on a phone and play it back, “send” it to each other, reply in voice. Their medium exactly, and hearing your own voice is a strong hook. One caution: this is their play, on their phones, separate from the session recorder — and anything recorded this way gets deleted in front of them if either girl wants it gone.

Run 1 as the spine and let 2 or 3 take over if the room offers it. Whichever runs, bury one two-step instruction inside the play — "take the pencil and put it on the chair" — because two-step comprehension is the cheapest A3 evidence there is.

Track B — the secret messages

B1 letters ≥5 of 6 · B2 decode 2 of 3 CVC · B3 one sentence read with meaning · always runs, whatever happened above

What this actually is: before they arrive, you write short English sentences in block capitals on strips of paper — one strip per girl — and hide them around the room. Each strip starts with that girl's name, so she recognizes her strip before she can read a word of it; recognizing your own name is free, and it buys the confidence to attack the rest of the line. Finding it is a treasure hunt. Reading it is B2/B3. Doing what it says is the proof that meaning arrived — which is the only version of a reading test that can't be faked by a good accent.

You say (Hebrew)יש פה שתי הודעות סודיות מוחבאות. אחת לכל אחת — עם השם שלכן עליה. ואסור לעזור אחת לשנייה. Two secret messages hidden here, one each, with your name on it — and no helping each other. The no-helping clause is what makes it a game, and it's also the only thing protecting the data.

What to write — three levels, pick one per girl

K — RUN!
DANIEL — SIT!Easiest (B1→B2). One three-letter word. She can act it out without explaining it, so a girl with almost no reading still finishes with a win.
K CAN JUMP.
DANIEL LIKES PIZZA.Middle (B2). CVC blending plus one loanword she already owns. Reading it out is the pass; no action required.
DANIEL — LOOK UNDER THE TABLE.The good one (B3). A message that makes something happen, with a second strip waiting where it sends her. Comprehension is proven by her feet, not by her explanation — and you can chain two or three.
K LOVES BROCCOLI.The protest one (B3). Something gloriously untrue. She reads it, objects — "זה לא נכון!" — and the objection is the pass. Meaning arrived; no comprehension question needed.
  • Block capitals, thick marker, one sentence per strip. No cursive, no lowercase — lowercase letterforms are a separate skill and this isn't the beat to test it.
  • Hide them findable in twenty seconds — under a cushion, taped under the table edge, inside the marker box, tucked in the new notebook. This is your house: you know exactly where a nine-year-old will look first, which is an advantage you wouldn't have had at theirs.

The dissolve — leave while they're still busy

No goodbye · no door · no ceremony · recorder off and in your hand

They're here until 11:00 and nobody's interest is served by you spending two hours with them. The failure mode isn't leaving — it's ending: a closing line, a thank-you, a walk to the door, and the morning gets a full stop that says the visitor's obligation is discharged. Instead, the session doesn't end. It gets handed over and it keeps running without you.

The mechanics, in order

  • 09:44 — sell session two, inside the activity. Not at a door, not as a farewell. "Thursday I'm bringing the harder version of the message game." Then keep doing whatever you're doing. The hook has to land while they're still holding something.
  • 09:45 — hand over the unfinished thing. It must be (a) theirs, (b) genuinely not done, (c) doable without you. Best candidates: decorate the notebook covers with the stamps · stamp a secret message for each other and hide it · copy the word list off the board into their notebooks in their own handwriting · finish the character on the whiteboard.
  • The sentence that makes leaving not a rejection: "אני חוזר עוד קצת לראות מה יצא — אל תראו לי עד שזה גמור." — I'll come back in a bit to see how it turned out; don't show me until it's finished. Now your absence is a condition of the game rather than a withdrawal of attention.
  • Stop the recorder, out loud, and take it with you. "סוגר את ההקלטה." Non-negotiable and non-negotiably visible — see ◉ Recording.
  • Leave mid-momentum, while hands are moving. Not during a lull, not after the last thing finished.
  • ~10:20 — re-enter for two minutes. Look at what they made, one specific and non-comparative sentence about each, and go again. Costs three minutes and buys the goodbye you deliberately skipped — and it's the moment their mother is most likely to overhear something good about her daughters.

That's a better ending than a shared game you supervise, and it's the only version that survives you walking out.

Recording — their mother said yes; the harder half is the girls

Consent from the adult who's responsible for them is necessary and it isn't sufficient. They're 9 and 12, it's their voices, and being asked — genuinely, with a refusal that costs nothing — is probably the most respectful thing either of them will experience this week. Do it at the table, before you press anything, in Hebrew, and then stop talking.

You say (Hebrew), then waitאמא שלכן כבר אמרה לי שזה בסדר מבחינתה, אבל זה הקול שלכן — אז אני שואל גם אתכן. אני רוצה להקליט את השעה הזאת בשביל עצמי, כדי לשמוע אחר כך מה פספסתי ולכתוב נכון את המילים שאתן כבר יודעות. זה יושב אצלי במחשב, אף אחד אחר לא שומע את זה, ואני מוחק את ההקלטה אחרי שאני מוציא ממנה את המילים. אם לא נעים לכן — תגידו לא, וזהו. באמת. שום דבר לא משתנה. Your mom already said it's fine with her, but it's your voice, so I'm asking you too. … If it's not comfortable — say no, and that's it. Really. Nothing changes. Then silence. Let the pause be as long as it needs to be; filling it is how a free choice quietly becomes an expected one.

The rules, and they're absolute

  • One “no” ends it. Not “then I'll only record your sister” — a recorder in a room records everyone in it. Either both girls are comfortable or the device stays off, and it's never raised again.
  • A hesitant yes is a no. Their mother is employed by your mother and you're teaching them for free; both facts make agreement cheaper than refusal. If it isn't easy and immediate, treat it as declined and move on without a flicker of disappointment on your face.
  • Visible device, on the table. A recorder you can see is a promise. A recorder in a pocket is a trick, even when the intent is identical.
  • Off the moment the session ends — and off in your hand, not left in the room. Beat 10 ● says exactly what you're giving up by honouring this.
  • Off for anything that isn't the lesson — bathroom, phone call, their mother stopping to chat. If she's in the room and it's running, she knows.
  • “Stop” is honoured instantly, from either girl, at any moment, with no question asked and no cost paid.
  • The file is never shared with anyone — not their mother, not your family. She consented to you recording a lesson, not to receiving audio of her daughters.
