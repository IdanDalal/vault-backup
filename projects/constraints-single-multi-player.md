# Constraints: single-player and multi-player

Thread opened on Idi's word, 2026-09-27. Proposal-grade: jep structures, Idi rules. Nothing here is a conclusion yet.

## Idi's words (as typed, 2026-09-27)

> invented constraints come in different shapes, and can have different purposes and effects. I think there are "Single-player" ones "Multi-player" ones. "Single-player" ones are destructive/harmful/separatist/dividing, they arise as a response to undesirable desires and include "I must do X alone, privately, secretly, because if I do it in public once, the environment will not allow me to do it again even once" (theory: Cobb seeing his kids, reality: criminals committing crime). "Multi-player" ones are constructive/valuable/uniting/clarifying, they arise from desirable desires and include "I must do X in this specific way, because if I do it any other way, either the environment or my own self will not allow me to do it forever".

## Where the tension came from

- I4 Level III: omnipotent dreamer, total freedom = paralysis, so Cobb invents constraints; the theory judges them as avoidance.
- TELOS Core Insight: signal-void, empty chair filled by recruitment, "build the forcing function before the goal"; invented constraints judged as the cure.
- Idi's split says both judgments hold, for different species.

## Candidate tests (jep proposals, Idi strikes or keeps)

- T1. Witness test: does anyone else know the constraint exists? Single-player constraints need secrecy to survive.
- T2. Exposure test: what happens to the constraint the first time someone sees it? Single-player breaks or shames; multi-player strengthens.
- T3. Conversion test: can a single-player constraint turn multi-player by adding a witness, keeping the same rule? (Cobb's elevator says yes; Ariadne never removes Mal, she only sees her.)
- T4. Direction test: does the constraint move toward the desire (multi-player) or hide the desire (single-player)?

## Open questions for Idi (answer any, in any order, or strike)

1. Is secrecy the defining trait, or is the source (undesirable desire) the defining trait? A private constraint born from a desirable desire (a diary, a solo practice) tests which one leads.
2. Your multi-player example says "the environment OR my own self will not allow me". The self-only version has no second player. Is it multi-player because of its source, or does the self count as a player?
3. Which of your current constraints are single-player? (No answer needed here; the question can stay open.)
4. Does the "empty chair" filled by recruitment describe the conversion in T3: a single-player life turned multi-player by recruiting a witness?
