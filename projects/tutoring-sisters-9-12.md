Two daughters of Mom's housekeeper, ages 9 and 12, who would massively benefit from improving their English. Idi has already talked with them directly and feels very positive about it. Free first lessons per the standing offer.

**State (2026-07-05):** contact made, mutual interest established; the open step is scheduling a first session. Idi's stated intent: begin this week.

**Reusable from [[tutoring-a-first-session]]:** the treasure-hunt diagnostic, notebook given minute one, 5–7 workhorse words + one wow-word per session, end-on-a-win, ~40-minute cap, the full risk playbook. ## First session: Thursday 2026-07-24 — both girls together (2-on-1)

**The open decision resolved itself by logistics** — their mom is bringing both on Thursday. So session one runs 2-on-1, and it doubles as the data source for the real together-vs-separate call (see decision rule at the bottom). The instrument is still [[tutoring-universal-baseline]] — one ladder, two climbers, two scoring sheets.

### Adaptations to the UAB

- **Two scoring sheets, one game.** Each girl climbs at her own rung *inside shared games* — never two visible tests running side by side in the same room. Scoring stays in your head, sheets filled after they leave, same as always.
- **Address every probe by name.** The single most predictable failure is the 12-year-old answering for the 9-year-old. Counter with a "microphone" prop (any object): only the holder answers. Introduce it as a game rule in minute one, before it's ever needed as a correction.
- **Treasure hunt runs twice inside one conversation** — each girl's own territory, and the bag carries two domain loads (already specified in the UAB). Time discipline: the sim predicted the hunt runs long with *one* kid; with two, cap it at ~12 minutes even if it's going great — the revelation ("you both already speak English") lands once per girl, then move.
- **A1 is natively multiplayer.** Simon Says with name-addressed commands ("דנה — jump! מאיה — touch something red!") produces clean per-girl receptive data while feeling like one shared game.
- **A2 role-reversal as a team:** "you two are the teachers now — each of you teach me words the other one didn't." The rivalry channel becomes coverage, not comparison.
- **The gap is an asset — promote the older to co-teacher.** When (likely: *if*) the 12-year-old's rung outpaces her sister's, have her deliver English instructions to the 9-year-old. Her production is A2/A3 data; the younger gets receptive reps; the age gap converts from hazard to engine.
- **Parallel stations when frontiers diverge hard:** one girl gets the drawing task (bag supplies; e.g. decorating the "secret message" strip) while the other gets the conversational rungs; then swap. A4 with a 12-year-old is just genuine conversation about her territory — quietest possible probe with a sibling in the room.

### Risks specific to 2-on-1 (on top of the inherited playbook)

- **Misses are socially expensive with an audience.** Back off a rung *faster* than you would 1-on-1 — one hesitant miss in front of a sibling costs more than one lost data point.
- **The 12-year-old's assessment radar, doubled:** being out-climbed by a little sister is the nightmare scenario. Never let their rungs be visibly compared; praise is individual, specific, and never relative ("that jump-command was instant" — not "you're faster than her").
- **Never end either girl's track on a miss** (UAB rule), and end the *session* on a joint win — both teaching you something together.
- **Mom will ask "who's better?"** Prepared answer: different doors, both walked through them — then one specific win per girl. Separate ledgers in the parent notes (skeleton rule), sent as separate messages even to the same phone.
- **No recording on Thursday.** The bake-off isn't validated and the transparency/consent conversation hasn't happened — and with the housekeeper power dynamic, consent must be genuinely refusable, which takes an unhurried conversation, not a doorstep ask. Hand-filled scoring sheets are enough for session one.

### Together-vs-separate — decision rule (apply to Thursday's data)

- Frontiers **≤1 rung apart** on both tracks → together is viable, possibly better: they co-regulate fear, and co-teacher structure works.
- Frontiers **≥2 rungs apart** on either track → recommend separate sessions to mom, framed as "each gets my full attention" — never as levels.
- Either way the girls' own preference, visible by minute 40, gets heavy weight.
