# Online research: agent-generated daily note (Do X → Idi did X)

Grades: study / meta-analysis / practitioner / vendor / anecdote. Confidence per line: high / medium / low. Paywalled abstracts marked "abstract unread" where the fetch returned 403.

## Q1. Daily-note structures that persist (2024 to 2026 PKM practice)

- Obsidian core Daily Notes + Periodic Notes + Templater: standard stack; users report building templates over months, changing small things, keeping what sticks. Forum thread "Daily Note Template; a minimal approach" (https://forum.obsidian.md/t/daily-note-template-a-minimal-approach/69295) - practitioner - high.
- Persisting pattern in Obsidian showcases: daily note as "homepage", first tab opened; only 2 to 3 sections need typed input (plan, new tasks, recap), rest auto-generated by queries. Jake Mahr template (https://medium.com/@jakeamahr/a-look-at-my-obsidian-daily-note-template-4093802ba2a2), dannb.org (https://dannb.org/blog/2022/obsidian-daily-note-template/) - practitioner - medium.
- Nick Milo / LYT: ACE framework (Atlas, Calendar, Effort); Calendar area = daily notes for "notes to future self", free-write, review. No prescription on daily template size found. Tara H summary (https://medium.com/@THLiterary/nick-milos-new-lyt-framework-how-to-ace-it-40f83942c48e) - practitioner - medium.
- Bullet Journal migration (Ryder Carroll): unfinished task rewritten by hand or struck; friction is the filter ("if an entry isn't worth the effort to rewrite, then it's probably not that important"); monthly at minimum, daily in practice; purpose stated as "separate the signal from the noise". bulletjournal.com/blogs/faq/migration - practitioner - high. Evidence: anecdote + book sales; no study.
- Interstitial journaling (Tony Stubblebine, 2017): timestamped one-liners at every task switch; "what I just did, what I do next"; replaces to-do list; credits Allen's next action and Pomodoro. Stubblebine founded Coach.me (coaching app). https://medium.com/better-humans/replace-your-to-do-list-with-interstitial-journaling-to-increase-productivity-4e43109d15ef - practitioner - high. Evidence: anecdote.
- Cal Newport time-block planner: daily metric line, tasks, ideas, "shutdown complete" checkbox; shutdown ritual = process open items, confirm each has a home, scan calendar, say the phrase. https://calnewport.com/the-time-blocking-revolution-begins/ - practitioner/vendor - high. Evidence: anecdote; the "close open loops" rationale leans on Masicampo & Baumeister (Q3).
- Flowstate / Rúnar, "How to Plan Your Day in 10 Minutes" (YouTube rS7PI_FfjrY): 1 to 3 dominoes, planning and doing in separate sittings, one release valve always in the same place. Vendor - high on what it prescribes. Productivity multipliers are the vendor's, unreplicated.
- Mark Forster closed list: draw a line at end of day; above the line = tomorrow's fixed scope, nothing added; open list = everything else. Burkeman restates it as "fixed volume", closed list of ten at most. Workflowy blog (https://blog.workflowy.com/how-to-use-a-closed-list-system-in-workflowy/), Forster forum (http://markforster.squarespace.com/forum/post/2784956) - practitioner - high. Evidence: anecdote.
- Ivy Lee method (1918, Charles Schwab story): six tasks max, written the night before, ranked, done in order, undone items move to tomorrow's six. Origin story lacks a verified primary source beyond Cutlip's PR history; treat figures as folklore. James Clear (https://jamesclear.com/ivy-lee), goalsandprogress (https://goalsandprogress.com/ivy-lee-method-remote-work/) - anecdote - high that it is folklore.
- What fails across all of the above (practitioner consensus): templates that demand input on empty days, streak guilt, no review pass, and lists that stay open. Sources in Q5.

## Q2. Rollover mechanics and task zombies

- Obsidian Rollover Daily Todos (lumoe): rolls `- [ ]` lines from the most recent past daily note into today under a chosen heading; options: delete from previous note, remove empty todos, roll children; conflicts with Templater auto-run (todos deleted but never written); no age counter, no cap. https://github.com/lumoe/obsidian-rollover-daily-todos - vendor - high.
- Sunsama: every unfinished task auto-rolls at midnight; recurring tasks collapse to a single instance; roadmap has a user request to stop rollover of incomplete recurring tasks, meaning rollover fatigue is a live complaint. https://help.sunsama.com/docs/task-rollover-and-recurring-tasks-the-basics, https://roadmap.sunsama.com/improvements/p/option-to-not-rollover-incomplete-repeating-tasks - vendor - high.
- Amazing Marvin: rollover optional; capacity bar from time estimates, learns personal averages, warns on over-planning. https://amazingmarvin.com/features/day-planning/ - vendor - high.
- Things 3: Today items do not auto-carry; support page says "reschedule the remaining items for another day"; Today is meant lean; "This Evening" section defers within the day. https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/4001304/ - vendor - high.
- OmniFocus: undone due-today items turn into overdue red; users describe "a sea of red overdue items" and bumping dates forward; deferred no-due tasks vanish from Forecast the day after. Inside OmniFocus (https://inside.omnifocus.com/peter-akkies2), forum (https://discourse.omnigroup.com/t/deferred-dates-forecast-and-overdue-tasks/7686) - practitioner - medium.
- Bullet Journal: rewrite or strike; each migration is a forced re-decision; no automatic carry. bulletjournal.com migration page - practitioner - high.
- Forster Autofocus dismissal rule: pass a page slowly; if nothing "stands out", every remaining item on that page is dismissed (highlighted, reviewed later); unfinished work gets re-entered at the list end ("little and often"). https://www.artofmanliness.com/character/behavior/autofocus-the-productivity-system-that-treats-your-to-do-list-like-a-river/ - practitioner - high. This is the closest prior art to "kill after N passes".
- Carroll on guilt: "Transform any guilt into curiosity by asking yourself why each task might still be incomplete" (quoted via search summary of bulletjournal.com; exact page not fetched) - practitioner - medium.
- Named pitfalls with sources: overdue accumulation (OmniFocus users above); rollover of recurring tasks (Sunsama roadmap); no aging signal in the Obsidian plugin (README). No study on "task zombies" found; all practitioner.

## Q3. Behavioral science, with what was measured vs what is claimed

- Implementation intentions ("if situation X, then I do Y"): Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, 94 tests, >8,000 participants, d = 0.65 on goal attainment vs goal intention alone. https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c4deb3507fe725ce6363c1735f1ba83bab20d665 - meta-analysis - high. Update: Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2024, 642 tests, effect varies by plan format and motivation (secondary source reports d 0.27 to 0.66); children d = 0.43 (Breitwieser). https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563 (abstract unread, 403); range via https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-research/ - meta-analysis - medium on the range. Measured: self-set goals, mostly health and study behaviors. Popular claim: any if-then works; actual: effect needs an existing goal intention, weak when motivation is absent.
- Masicampo & Baumeister 2011 "Consider It Done": making a specific plan for an unfinished goal removed its intrusion on a later task; completion itself was unnecessary. JPSP 101(4) - study - high on existence; replication status unknown to me - low. Popular claim: "GTD closes open loops"; measured: lab tasks, intrusive thoughts, within one session.
- Zeigarnik effect (unfinished tasks remembered better): 2025 meta-analysis, 37 studies excluding Zeigarnik's own, recall ratio interrupted:completed = 0.99, no memory advantage; Ovsiankina effect (urge to resume interrupted tasks) does hold. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w (redirect, abstract via search) - meta-analysis - high. Under a third of 44 earlier replication papers found the effect (Springer 2020 review, https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-020-01033-5) - study - high. Verdict: do not build on Zeigarnik; the resumption urge is the usable part.
- Planning fallacy: Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994, thesis students predicted 33.9 days, took 55.5. https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f91964dad8c0e54cd58b1aa99e430b900fcf082b - study - high. Unpacking (listing sub-steps) reduces it: Kruger & Evans 2004 JESP - study - medium; effect size not extracted. Robust across many replications in general (Flyvbjerg project literature, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00125) - high.
- Fresh start effect: Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014 Management Science, archival: diet searches, gym visits, commitment-contract signups rise after week/month/year starts and birthdays. https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901 - study - high. Lab follow-up 2015 Psych Science. Preregistered 2025 test (Milyavskaya et al., N = 362, January vs March goals) exists; results paywalled, abstract unread. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00332941251399129 - study - low on direction. Measured: aggregate behavior counts at landmarks; popular claim: "Monday makes you motivated"; the effect is on initiation, and a later paper shows anticipated landmarks can undermine continued pursuit (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342997839) - medium.
- Progress principle: Amabile & Kramer 2011, diary study, 238 people, 26 teams, 7 companies, ~12,000 entries; best-day predictor = progress on meaningful work; setbacks weigh 2 to 3 times more than progress. https://hbr.org/2011/05/the-power-of-small-wins - study (correlational diary) - high on design. Measured: knowledge workers' daily mood vs events; popular claim: "celebrate small wins"; the causal direction is inferred, no RCT.
- Habit formation: Lally et al. 2010, N = 96 started, asymptotic curve fit only 39 of 82 analyzed; among those, median 66 days, range 18 to 254. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674 - study - high. Keller et al. 2021 RCT: median 59 days to 95% asymptote among those who formed a habit. https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjhp.12504 - study - high. Popular claim: "66 days"; measured: self-rated automaticity of one self-chosen cue-behavior pair; half the sample never fit the curve.
- Self-affirmation: Sweeney & Moyer 2015, d = 0.26 intentions, d = 0.27 behavior; Epton et al. 2015, 144 tests, d ≈ 0.32 behavior. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25089345/ - meta-analysis - high. Small, in health-message contexts; not a daily-note ingredient.
- Self-determination theory, controlling vs autonomy-supportive instruction: SDT interventions raise autonomous motivation g = 0.41, autonomy satisfaction g = 0.81 (Ntoumanis et al. 2020 health meta-analysis, https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17437199.2020.1718529) - meta-analysis - high. Two techniques with larger effects at follow-up: non-controlling language and a meaningful rationale. Controlling language = "should", "must", "have to"; supportive = "you may", "feel free", rationale given (Vansteenkiste et al. 2004; Reeve 2009, https://www.csuchico.edu/freespeech/_assets/documents/pedagogy/reeve,-j-2009.pdf) - study - high on the classification, medium on magnitude for adults outside classrooms.
- Directly on point, AI-authored goals: Chi, Rietsche, Göldi, Ungar & Guntuku 2026, preregistered, N = 470, follow-up N = 332. LLM goals scored higher on SMART quality (d = 2.26) yet lower psychological ownership (d = 1.38), commitment (d = 1.19), importance (d = 1.13). Two weeks later 72.8% of self-authors acted on 2+ goals vs 46.6% for LLM goals. Ownership mediated everything; goal quality mediated nothing. Erosion 55% larger for low self-efficacy users. No co-authoring condition tested. Authors recommend scaffolded articulation and "refinement over generation". https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12344 - study - high (single preregistered study, no replication yet).

## Q4. Neurodivergence-specific planning guidance

- Barkley (ADHD): internally generated motivation is weak; externalize time, information, and motivation "at the point of performance"; shorten gaps between cue, act, and consequence; faster and more immediate feedback. https://www.russellbarkley.org/factsheets/ADHD_EF_and_SR.pdf, CADDRA 20 principles (https://adhdtreat.caddra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Barkley-CADDAC-2025-treat.pdf) - practitioner with theory base - high on consensus, medium on effect evidence (mostly children).
- Body doubling: practitioner consensus strong, research thin; one controlled study reports faster completion and better sustained attention with human or AI body doubles; 2025 to 2026 VR/MR design papers exist. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12153, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07851 - study (small) - medium. A resident agent visibly present counts as a possible body double; untested claim - low.
- Autism, visual schedules: literature review (2024) on visual schedules and on-task behavior; predictability lowers anxiety, working-memory demand drops when the plan is external. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20473869.2024.2402124 - study review - medium (mostly school-age, single-case designs). Executive-function difficulty prevalence in autism quoted at 35 to 90% depending on measure (PMC9405696) - medium.
- Practitioner consensus for both ADHD and ASD: reduce choices, one visible next step, time anchors, break into mini-tasks, keep routines identical day to day. autismbc.ca, meristem.pro - practitioner - high on consensus, low on evidence quality.
- Masicampo & Baumeister (Q3) supports "a concrete plan calms the open loop", useful for OCD-flavored rumination; no OCD-specific test - low.

## Q5. Why daily systems get abandoned

- Guilt spiral: "Miss a day, feel bad. Miss two days, feel worse"; filler entries on empty days; work split across days loses context; monthly review of 30 daily files never happens. Hunter Wiginton, dev.to (https://dev.to/hackastak/weekly-notes-beat-daily-notes-the-sustainable-way-to-never-forget-anything-kj2) - anecdote - high. His rules: capture under 10 seconds, system may change without resistance, "usable messiness beats perfect but abandoned".
- Esteban Thilliez, "Why I finally stopped using daily notes" (https://medium.com/@estebanthi/why-i-finally-stopped-using-daily-notes-e6400f153e4e) - anecdote - low (403, title only).
- Journaling attrition: ~8% keep a journal, ~22% tried and quit (survey figure, source uncited in the blog). Mario Mosca, Medium - anecdote - low on the numbers.
- "Journaling shouldn't feel like homework": streaks add consequences for failing without lowering effort. https://blog.deariary.com/posts/2026-03-19-journaling-shouldnt-feel-like-homework - vendor - medium.
- Sustainable budget: under 15 min/day, under 1 hour/week on the system itself, else net negative. whennotesfly.com checklist - practitioner - medium. Matches the vault's emissary ratio.
- Carroll's migration (Q1) is itself an anti-bloat mechanism; Forster's dismissal (Q2) is another. Both practitioner.
- Ownership loss (Q3, Chi et al. 2026) is the one abandonment cause with a preregistered study behind it, and it applies directly to agent-written instructions.

## Q6. Second-person instruction rewritten as third-person journal: prior art

- Franklin: morning question "What good shall I do this day?", evening "What good have I done today?"; same frame, tense flipped; hourly schedule in the Autobiography. https://www.good.is/articles/what-good-shall-i-do-this-day-asked-benjamin-franklin-every-single-morning - practitioner (historical) - high.
- Ivy Lee: tonight's six become tomorrow's order; undone go to the next list. Folklore (Q1) - anecdote.
- Interstitial journaling: "Do next" and "just did" lines coexist in one timestamped stream; Coach.me built coach prompts in second person on top of it. Stubblebine (Q1) - practitioner - high.
- Distanced self-talk (Kross et al. 2014, JPSP, 7 studies, N = 585): own name or "you" instead of "I" increases self-distance, lowers stress in first-impression and speech tasks. https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/emotion-selfcontrol-psych/wp-content/uploads/sites/1322/2024/07/KrossJ_Pers_Soc_Psychol2014Self-talk_as_a_regulatory_mechanism_How_you_do_it_matters.pdf - study - high.
- Replication: Orvell et al. 2021, two high-powered experiments, works across intensity levels and in chronically distressed people. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2167702620951539 - study - high. Moser et al. 2017 ERP, d = 0.87 on a neural marker, N = 29; ERP replication paper 2022. - study - medium (small N).
- Meta-analysis: Murdoch et al. 2023, Stress and Health 39(2), self-distanced vs self-immersed reflection, "small-to-moderate advantage" on negative emotion; authors rate overall evidence quality as uncertain. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/smi.3199 (abstract unread, 403) - meta-analysis - medium. Measured: brief lab reflections on past stressors; popular claim: "talk to yourself in third person to perform better". Side effect: distanced self-talk increased self-interested choices in a dictator game (N = 774, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-04010-3) - study - high.
- Daily-life test 2025: distanced self-talk improved momentary affect in one situation type, preparing what to say or do; other situation types showed no benefit. news-medical summary (https://www.news-medical.net/news/20251109/...) - study - medium.
- No prior art found for an agent rewriting "Do X" into "Idi did X" as a daily log; the nearest are Franklin's tense flip, Ivy Lee's carry-forward, and Coach.me's coach voice. The Chi et al. 2026 study is the only evidence about who writes the imperative and it argues for Idi's authorship of the goal and the agent's authorship of the refinement.

## Design implications

1. Idi authors the domino in his words; jep refines, orders, and adds the if-then; never generates the goal from scratch (Chi et al. 2026, ownership d = 1.38, action rate 73% vs 47%).
2. Each "Do X" line carries a one-clause rationale and a choice point ("A or B, jep suggests A"); non-controlling verbs, no "must", "should", "have to" (Reeve 2009; Ntoumanis 2020).
3. Write each instruction as if-then: cue + place + action ("After coffee, at the HQ desk, open X and do Y"), the format with d ≈ 0.65 (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006).
4. Cap at 3, ordered; first item is the one that unblocks others (Ivy Lee, Flowstate, Burkeman's fixed volume).
5. Rollover with an age counter: an item that rolls twice gets a forced re-decision line on the third morning ("kill, schedule, or shrink to one step"); Forster's dismissal rule and Carroll's rewrite friction, both practitioner.
6. Evening rewrite uses distanced third person ("Idi did X") only for the log; keep instructions in second person. Expect a small-to-moderate calming effect at most (Murdoch 2023); do not sell it as performance.
7. Log setbacks with one neutral line and no streak count; setbacks weigh 2 to 3 times more than wins (Amabile & Kramer 2011).
8. Undone item gets a planned next step written by jep, since a concrete plan quiets the loop as well as completion does (Masicampo & Baumeister 2011).
9. Unpack any item over one sitting into its first physical step; the planning fallacy shrinks with enumeration (Kruger & Evans 2004).
10. Never require input on an empty day; the note stays valid with zero typing (Wiginton; deariary).
11. Use week and month starts as opt-in relaunch days without any deadline attached; the fresh-start effect is about initiation, and anticipated landmarks can sap ongoing effort.
12. Budget the whole ritual at under 15 minutes of Idi time per day, per the practitioner ceiling and the vault's emissary ratio.

## Pitfalls to design against

1. Zombie tasks: no age counter means items roll forever (Obsidian plugin has none; OmniFocus "sea of red").
2. Ownership loss from agent-written goals; higher SMART quality coexisted with lower ownership and lower follow-through (Chi et al. 2026).
3. Guilt from gaps and streaks; a blank day must cost nothing (Wiginton).
4. Building on the Zeigarnik effect; it does not replicate (2025 meta-analysis, ratio 0.99).
5. Template weight; every mandatory section is a reason to skip the note (Thilliez, Wiginton).
6. Notes nobody rereads; the rewrite step has to be the review, or no review happens.
7. Controlling voice in second-person lines; "must" and "should" convert the note into duty (Vansteenkiste 2004).
8. Overclaiming small effects: self-affirmation d ≈ 0.27, self-distancing small-to-moderate, body doubling one small study; cite them as nudges.
