## Idi's two principles (his words, condensed only where marked)

**P1. The oldest scenes:** "some of the 'old'/first scenes we generated weren't ambitious enough and now they highlight the difference in 'production value'/'complexity' between them and the new/latest 'ceiling-level' scenes. Therefore, we should reexamine the scenes that have changed the least since v1."

**P2. Iterate, never copy (the grappling hook):** "we replicate/duplicate elements/techniques/concepts from the refs (especially the new/latest 'ceiling-level' ones), rather than iterate/evolve them. ... Batman's grappling hook in Arkham Knight is VERY different than Crane's grappling hook in Dying Light - they both exist in different environments, solve different problems, and interact with different systems. Knowledge Cutoff is like either of the games: it'd be a worse game without the grappling hook ... and if you copy-paste the grappling hook from the other game into it, it'd be even worse. The only solution is to combine and modify the elements/techniques/concepts to create new ones of our own - not 'create an entire kinetic movement system from scratch and reinvent traveling mechanics', more like 'make our own grappling hook'."
