## 2. Efficiency (note 1): what v3 measures

1. **Things enter on the music and leave off it.** Objects other than lyric words, whole film:

| event | how | count | on a music cue | chance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| entry | appears in place | 120 | 81 % | 38 % |
| entry | moves in | 201 | 63 % | 42 % |
| exit | vanishes in place | 104 | 51 % | 55 % |
| exit | moves out | 125 | 22 % | 49 % |
| exit | leaves over the frame edge | 157 | 32 % | 43 % |

   Music cue = a beat, a strong kick or a sung word's start within 50 ms. Chance = the same test on the same events shifted in time. Limit: for a moving exit the capture times the last frame, where the cue would sit on the start of the move. Film rule for v4, in Idi's words: "everything enters and leaves on cue".
2. **55 of 722 lyric words** are still on screen 6 s after they are sung (median stay 2.0 s). Longest: the games tab bar (INFINITE LIVES, GOD MODE ON, SPEEDRUNNERS) 21-27 s; the exam's NOW ASK ME WHY 8.3-9.1 s; chorus 2's first line 7.8-9.5 s; the kitchen's SO, WHAT'S A HALF OF A HALF 7.7-8.9 s; the outro's PAY ATTENTION, OR PAY THE PRICE 7.1-9.0 s.
3. **98 objects and 12 object families** stay 6 s or more. Largest: the games shelf and trophies 10.7 s (motionless 85-94 % of it), the kitchen's bars and clock 11 s, the outro's box and strip 11 s.
4. **Idi's pinned note on the essay, measured:** the essay cards hold 0:58.2-1:09.1 (10.9 s); the trick prompts show for 0.9 s where reading needs 1.5 s. The blind critics found the same stretch with no hint (third-strongest finding of the first half). New: the same essay text is on screen up to 18 times at 0:36.9-0:41.4, one plate earlier.
5. **Repeats:** 80 rows of a word on screen more than once (29 more echo the sung line, mostly by design). Across plates: the receipt's credit block is read three times (open 2 s, chorus 2 10 s, outro 9 s); the bulb-grid picture of 3:50 returns unchanged at 4:10; the kitchen's five labels return unchanged at 4:12.
6. **Too brief to read:** 11 strings (class 4, late 2, outro 5), against 0.25 s a word + 0.5 s.
7. **One focus** (blind critics): 55 moments where two or more things pull the eye. Strongest: 2:52.5-2:55.0 kitchen (four quadrants active), 1:40.0-1:42.5 fact, 3:53.5 verse 4 (seven kinds of thing in one frame), 3:05.0-3:13.5 kitchen (the lamp flare is the largest thing while the reading is small beside it).
8. **Strays:** the cut-off "or" outside the games border for 33 s (blind critic's strongest finding; known since 11-12; confirmed by jep's eye at 2:10). Type cut by a frame edge at 38 moments, 12 of them for 1 s or longer; the rest are single frames inside a camera move, so each order says "check at 30 fps". Of all 222 findings the critics marked 66 high confidence, 101 medium, 55 low.
9. **The two methods agree.** Every strong critic finding the engine can see (essay, tab bar, trophies, exam hold, kitchen labels, callback list) is also an audit row.
10. **One false lead, caught by eye:** the engine draws WHAT DO YOU KNOW? through all of games (34.4 s); at 2:10 it is hidden. Rule: a text-time row needs an eye before it becomes an order.

## 4. The PC's own models, measured

1. **Tool writing** (the rigidity tool, to a spec and a 29-check gate). Attempt 1, thinking on: 6.6 min, hit its 16,000-token cap mid-file, server error, nothing written. Attempt 2, thinking off: a file in about 1 min that passed 28 of 29 checks; check 29 was jep's test error. The worker then looped 10 min on that check and overwrote its passing file with a debug script. Kept: 2 of 6 functions. jep wrote the rest; the gate passes 29 of 29.
2. Lessons for the worker: thinking off; a scratch file of its own; the harness keeps every version it writes and stops it at the gate-run cap; jep's gate runs against a reference before the worker sees it.
3. **Frame reading:** one 1080p frame a second, 292 frames in 39 min (6.2 s a frame), 5 unparseable. 97.4 % of the words it read were really drawn (92-99 % per plate), so it rarely invents text. It read 40 % of the engine's words of 4+ letters: a floor, since the engine's list holds hidden draws. Its "cut text" flags: 154 texts in 75 separate seconds. It caught 26 of the critics' 38 edge cuts and found the standing "or" in games by itself (runs of 9, 5, 5 and 4 s). 32 flagged seconds are its alone; jep's eye on one (2:54, ASLEEP) found a real cut, made by an overlapping bubble, now ruling `kitchen-e06`. Use: a list of seconds to look at, read at full size where the critics had thumbnails.
4. **Sizing:** in this phase scripts on engine data did the measuring. Tool writing cost jep more tokens (spec, gate, rescue) than it saved. Frame reading paid: 39 min of the card for a look-here list, at the cost of three short scripts and one frame for jep's eye. The plan's 5-10 % saving stays unproven.
