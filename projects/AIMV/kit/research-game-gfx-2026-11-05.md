# Game-graphics tricks for the Pay Attention engine (r-gfx, 2026-11-05)

Question: which tricks game engines use for detail and immersion can our offline music-video engine learn from? Engine read on 2026-11-05: `D:\work\aimv-lab\pay-attention` (paths below are relative to `app\src\`). Look: treatment section 3 (look A, one white key light, greyscale materials, letter-shaped shadows, screen-blue as emitted light only) and the R31 rules 1-7.

## 0. What the engine is, in game terms

1. **A 2.5D deferred-ish renderer with an offline budget.** PA plates draw on Canvas 2D layers; `persp()` (`scenes/_pa.ts:171`) projects world points through a pinhole camera; `lightPass()` (`scenes/_pa.ts:244`) lights the finished layer in screen space (falloff, letter shadows, AO, hatching, rims). There is no depth buffer and no normal buffer for the 2D layer: every screen-space trick below that games drive from depth or normals has to get its depth from what `persp()` already knows (the plate's own z values). Confidence high (read the code).
2. **The three.js depth `Stage`** (`scenes/_kc-depth.ts`) has real depth, a 40-tap defocus and fog, but only the kit demos use it (`kitcamera.ts`, `kitdepth.ts`, `kitlook.ts`); no PA plate does (grep for `Stage(` across `scenes\*.ts`, 2026-11-05). The 3D-look ban (treatment 2.3) is one reason.
3. **The sub-frame accumulator is the big asset.** Every frame already averages 4 to 324 sub-frames (`engine/engine.ts:39-51`, loop `:365-522`). Film renderers get depth of field, soft shadows and motion blur from the same loop by jittering lens and light per sample (Cook, Porter, Carpenter 1984; Haeberli and Akeley 1990). Games approximate these in screen space because they cannot afford the samples. We can.

## 1. Glossary (48 terms)

Columns: what it is; what it adds (the perceptual reason it reads as real); real-time cheap version vs what offline affords; ours now; fit for PA (H/M/L, reason); cost to build (S/M/L engine work).

| # | term | what it is | what it adds | real-time vs offline | ours now | fit | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ambient occlusion (AO) | darkening where surfaces crowd each other so less ambient light reaches them | grounds objects: a thing touching a surface reads as touching | RT: estimate from the depth buffer per pixel; offline: trace hemisphere rays | yes, distance-blind: a 5 px blur ring of every caster cut from the wall (`scenes/_pa.ts:290`) | H: treatment 3 asks for AO where things meet | S to improve |
| 2 | SSAO | screen-space AO: samples the depth buffer around each pixel (Crytek, Crysis 2007) | cheap crevice darkening | RT: 8-16 noisy samples + blur; leaves halos at depth edges | our ring is SSAO's 2D cousin | H | - |
| 3 | HBAO+ / GTAO | better screen-space AO: march the depth "horizon" in a few directions (HBAO, NVIDIA 2008); GTAO matches a ray-traced reference at 0.5 ms (Activision 2016) | softer, correctly weighted contact darkness | RT: half-res + temporal filter; offline: no need, trace instead | no | M: needs per-mark height above the wall, which `persp` plates know | M |
| 4 | RTAO / MXAO / RTGI | ray-traced AO in games; MXAO and RTGI are Marty McFly's ReShade (iMMERSE) AO and ray-marched GI from the game's depth buffer only | what Idi sees when he turns RTGI on: corners fill with bounce and shade | ReShade guesses back faces ("object thickness"); offline knows them | no | M | - |
| 5 | Contact shadow | a short, hard shadow right where an object meets a surface | kills "floating": the eye reads contact from that 1-3 px dark line | RT: short screen-space ray march toward the light | yes: 0.7 px blurred copy of the casters (`scenes/_pa.ts:269`) | H: treatment 3 names it | - |
| 6 | Soft shadow / penumbra | the blurred fringe of a shadow, wider the farther the shadow falls from its caster and the bigger the light | tells the eye the light's size and the gap between object and surface | RT: blur the shadow map; offline: sample many points on the light | faked: 2 blur steps split by distance from the light (`scenes/_pa.ts:270-277`) | H: treatment 3 "hard close, soft far" | M (see top 10 #2) |
| 7 | PCSS | percentage-closer soft shadows: search blockers, then widen the shadow filter by their distance (Fernando, NVIDIA 2005) | penumbra that grows from the contact point | RT: 2 extra texture searches per pixel | no; our throw-based blur is a 2-step stand-in | H | M |
| 8 | Ray-traced shadows | shadows from rays cast toward an area light | exact penumbrae, no acne or leaks | RT: 1 ray + denoiser; offline: many rays, no denoiser | no | H (via light jitter, top 10 #2) | M |
| 9 | Depth of field (DOF) | blur of things nearer or farther than the focus plane, as a real lens does | depth and attention: the eye goes where it is sharp | RT: gather blur by circle-of-confusion from depth, halos at edges; offline: jitter the lens per sample (accumulation-buffer DOF) | `Stage` only: 40-tap gather (`scenes/_kc-depth.ts:104-121`); `Defocus` Gaussian layer blur (`scenes/_kc-glsl.ts:183`); none in PA plates | H: the video is about where attention goes | M (top 10 #1) |
| 10 | Bokeh | the shape out-of-focus lights take: aperture discs or polygons, cat's-eye near frame edges | strongest single "shot on a lens" cue | RT: sprite per bright pixel or a gather (CoD:AW 2014); offline: falls out of lens jitter | no | H: her "?" lights out of focus become discs | S with #9 |
| 11 | Focus pull / rack focus | moving the focus plane from one subject to another during a shot | directs attention in time, a cinema grammar | RT: animate the focus distance | no | H: pull from her ? to the cursor on a beat | S with #9 |
| 12 | Tilt-shift | a tilted focus plane that makes a scene read as a miniature | toy scale | RT: blur by screen y | no | L: miniaturises the room; nothing in the treatment calls for it | S |
| 13 | Camera motion blur | blur from the camera's own movement during the shutter | speed and weight of a move | RT: blur along a camera-velocity field; Digital Trends calls it cheap and often ugly | yes, exact: the frame camera is warped per sub-frame (`engine/engine.ts:617-656`) and accumulated | H: camera honesty (rule 3) | - |
| 14 | Per-object motion blur | blur from each object's own motion | slams and drops read as fast and physical | RT: velocity buffer + reconstruction filter (McGuire 2012), artifacts at overlaps; offline: accumulate sub-frames | yes, exact for every pixel (adaptive 4-324 sub-frames) | H | - |
| 15 | Shutter angle | the share of the frame interval the shutter is open: 180 degrees = half (film's default) | 180 reads "cinema"; 45-90 reads staccato (war films); 360 reads smeared, video-like | RT: scale the velocity | yes: `--shutter` and `still(t, samples, shutter)` (ENGINE.md row "fixed samples"); one value per render today (inference from ENGINE.md, medium) | H: a per-shot ladder is free | S (top 10 #10) |
| 16 | Volumetric lighting | light scattering in air (haze, dust) so the light's volume itself glows, with shadows cut through it | the light becomes a physical thing in the room | RT: froxel grid ray-march, 160x90x64 cells at 1.1 ms (Wronski, AC4 2014); offline: ray-march per pixel at full res | no | M: motivated air only (chalk dust, steam); beams as decoration are banned (treatment 2.2) | M (top 10 #8) |
| 17 | God rays / light shafts | visible streaks where an occluder blocks a light in haze | drama, source direction | RT: radial blur of the occluder mask toward the light on screen (Mitchell, GPU Gems 3 ch. 13, 2007) | no, though `lightPass` already builds the occluder mask `C` (`scenes/_pa.ts:251`) | M: same ban risk as #16 | S |
| 18 | Fog / height fog | contrast falling with distance or below a height | depth and scale | RT: per-pixel exponential by depth | ad hoc: `fog(dz)` in `scenes/late.ts:444`; `Stage` fog (`scenes/_kc-depth.ts:105-107`) | H: one shared rule keeps all views agreeing (rule 2) | S (top 10 #7) |
| 19 | Atmospheric / aerial perspective | far things lose contrast and shift toward the air's colour (Leonardo's observation; Hillaire 2020 for the real-time sky) | the strongest monocular depth cue after occlusion and size | RT: lookup tables; ours: greyscale, so fade toward ink | partly: per-plate fog functions | H | S |
| 20 | Bloom | bright light spilling into neighbouring pixels (lens and eye scatter) | tells "this is brighter than white" | RT: mip-chain blur of pixels over a threshold | yes: 7 mips, threshold 1.0 (`engine/post.ts:81`, `:62-65`) | H: treatment 3.3 allows it on sources only; threshold above paper enforces that | - |
| 21 | Halation | red-orange glow round highlights from light bouncing off film's back layer | film stock feel | RT: tinted, wider bloom | yes, amber, on by default 0.12 (`engine/post.ts:157`, `:66`); no PA scene overrides it (grep, 2026-11-05) | L as is: an amber tint contradicts look A's monochrome white (treatment 3); probe before trusting this | S (fix: 0 or neutral) |
| 22 | Lens flare | ghost shapes and streaks from light reflecting between lens elements (Hullin et al. 2011 for the physical version) | "a camera filmed this" | RT: sprites along the light-to-centre line | no | L: banned except a 1-3 frame hit (treatment 2.4) | S |
| 23 | Chromatic aberration (CA) | colour fringes at frame edges from a lens bending wavelengths differently | cheap lens realism | RT: offset R and B samples | yes: radial R/B offset, default 0.8 px at the edge, always on (`engine/post.ts:147-152`, `:67`) | L as a constant: treatment 2.4 bans it except a 1-3 frame hit; in a greyscale look any fringe is the only colour besides screen-blue | S (fix: 0 by default) |
| 24 | Vignette | darkening toward the frame corners (lens falloff) | pulls the eye to the centre, hides empty corners | RT: radial multiply | yes, 0.28 (`engine/post.ts:165-167`) | M: fine as lens falloff; strong values read as Instagram | - |
| 25 | Film grain | per-frame noise like film's silver grains | texture in flat darks, hides banding, "filmed" | RT: hashed noise | yes, two scales, mid-tone weighted, seedable (`engine/post.ts:170-177`) | H | - |
| 26 | HDR rendering | computing light in linear values above 1.0 before squeezing to the screen | lights can be far brighter than paper, so bloom and falloff behave | RT: half-float targets | yes: HalfFloat linear targets (`engine/gl.ts:39-51`) | H | - |
| 27 | Tonemapping (filmic, ACES, AgX) | the curve that maps HDR values to the display: a toe in the darks, a shoulder in the highlights; ACES is the film industry's standard (Narkowicz's 2016 game fit); AgX is Blender 4.0's default, which desaturates bright colours toward white more gracefully | rich blacks and highlights that roll off instead of clipping | same cost RT and offline (one curve) | partial: a shoulder only, identity below 0.72 (`engine/post.ts:7-14`); no toe | H: treatment 3.2 wants true black to true white with 5+ readable greys | S (top 10 #3) |
| 28 | Exposure / eye adaptation | overall brightness; adaptation = the camera or eye adjusting over a second or two after a light change | stepping from dark into lamp light "feels" like eyes adjusting | RT: average luminance, eased; ours must be a pure function of t | uniform exists (`engine/post.ts:158`), no adaptation curve | H: the treatment's exposure ladder is literally this | S (top 10 #3) |
| 29 | Colour grading LUT | a lookup table that remaps every colour to a grade (ReShade's LUT.fx) | one consistent "stock" | RT: one 3D texture lookup | no | M: greyscale grade = a tone curve; a LUT adds little beyond #27 | S |
| 30 | Supersampling (SSAA / MSAA) | rendering several samples per pixel and averaging; MSAA does it only at geometry edges | clean edges on thin type and hairlines | RT: 2-8x edge samples; offline: render at 2x and rotate taps | yes: `--scale 2` plus `SS_TAP` rotated taps (`engine/gl.ts:23-31`) | H | - |
| 31 | Post AA (FXAA, SMAA) | edge-detect the finished image and blend along edges (FXAA by Lottes 2009; SMAA by Jimenez et al. 2012) | hides stair-steps cheaply | RT: one or two passes; blurs fine text | no, and we need none | L: would soften 3 px hairlines | - |
| 32 | TAA / DLAA | temporal AA: jitter the camera each frame and blend with reprojected history (Karis, UE4 2014); DLAA = NVIDIA's neural version at native res | smooth edges and shimmer-free detail | RT: history buffer, ghosting and smear on motion | no; our sub-frame accumulation is the offline equivalent without history | L: history reuse would break pure-function-of-t frames | - |
| 33 | DLSS / FSR / upscaling | render at lower res, reconstruct to 4K with a neural (DLSS) or hand-written (FSR) upscaler | frame rate at 4K | RT only | no | L: we have no frame-time budget; native 4K is the receipt | - |
| 34 | Sharpening (CAS) | contrast-adaptive sharpening: sharpens low-contrast pixels more than high (AMD FidelityFX, Lottes 2019) | restores crispness after TAA or upscaling | RT: one pass | no | L: our edges are already native; sharpening rings round type | - |
| 35 | Normal / bump map | a texture that tells the lighting a flat surface has bumps (bump = heights, normal = directions) | surface detail that reacts to the light: paper fibre, wood grain | RT: one texture fetch per pixel | no (hatching and engraving carry surface detail: `scenes/_kc-look.ts:355`, `:479`) | M-H: raking lamp light across paper fibre is 4K detail that obeys the key light | M (top 10 #9) |
| 36 | Parallax / parallax occlusion mapping (POM) | shifting a texture by view angle and height so a flat surface fakes depth; POM ray-marches the height map (Tatarchuk 2006) | carved, embossed surfaces | RT: 8-32 steps per pixel; silhouettes stay flat | layer parallax exists (`scenes/_kc-camera.ts:348`, `scenes/_kc-look.ts:227`), surface POM does not | M: debossed letters in a desk or a receipt's impact print | M |
| 37 | Tessellation, geometry and mesh shaders | GPU stages that create or cull geometry on the fly (subdivide a surface, emit lines, build meshlets) | dense geometry without CPU cost | RT only matters at frame-time budgets | equivalent: GPU line batches (`engine/lines.ts`) | L: our geometry is type and lines; no budget pressure | - |
| 38 | PBR (albedo, roughness, metalness) | physically based materials: albedo = base colour, roughness = how blurred reflections are, metalness = metal or not (Karis 2013, Lagarde 2014) | every material reacts to light consistently, so lighting changes never break it | same cost both | no: materials are greyscale tokens lit by `lightPass` | M: a roughness value per surface (paper rough, glass smooth) is the useful slice; full PBR drifts toward the banned Blender look | M |
| 39 | Specular highlight + Fresnel | the bright reflection of the light itself on a surface, stronger at grazing angles (Fresnel) | tells shininess and the light's position; moves with the camera, which proves 3D | RT: GGX lobe per light | partly: rims and glints on edges facing a rim light (`scenes/_pa.ts:322-331`); none moves with the camera | H: treatment 3 asks for glints on paper and glass | S-M (top 10 #6) |
| 40 | Screen-space reflections (SSR) | reflections built by marching the current frame's pixels | wet floors and glossy desks | RT: cheap; anything off-screen cannot reflect and fades at edges | no | L as SSR (edge fade breaks rule 2); see #41 | - |
| 41 | Planar / ray-traced reflections | redraw the scene mirrored through a flat surface (planar), or trace rays (RT) | a reflection that agrees with the room proves the room exists | RT: planar costs a second render; offline: fine | one bespoke case: the pupil mirror (R31 worked example) | H: rule 2 says "a reflection is the same place mirrored" | M (top 10 #5) |
| 42 | Refraction and caustics | light bending through glass or water (refraction) and the bright patterns it focuses on a surface (caustics) | glassware and water read as real | RT: screen-space offset; caustics usually baked; offline: photon or ray tracing | no | L-M: only if a glass of water or a lens object appears; caustics need a visible source by rule 5, which the lamp provides | M |
| 43 | Subsurface scattering (SSS) | light entering a material, spreading, leaving elsewhere (skin, wax, backlit paper) | soft translucent glow | RT: separable screen blur, under 0.5 ms (Jimenez 2015) | no | M: a page backlit by the lamp showing its reverse text is a cheap, rule-true detail | S |
| 44 | Global illumination (GI, light bounce) | light reflected off one surface lighting another | fills shadows with soft light from lit surfaces, the main "photoreal" cue | RT: probes, voxels, ReSTIR; offline: path tracing | no: shadows go to true black where the key misses | H: a lit page is a real source by rule 5 | S (top 10 #4) |
| 45 | Emissive surfaces | surfaces that emit light (screens, signs) and light their surroundings | screens glow onto faces and desks | RT: bloom only, or count them as GI sources | partly: screen-blue is drawn as emitted, `lightPass` lights carry `color` (`scenes/_pa.ts:202`) | H: treatment 3, screen-blue only as emitted light | S |
| 46 | LOD | swapping to simpler models at distance | frame rate | RT only; its pop-in is the classic artifact | no, not needed | L, and its pop breaks rule 1 | - |
| 47 | Decals | textures projected onto surfaces (bullet holes, stickers, stains) | wear and history | same both | the GOLD STAR sticker is drawn as one (`scenes/late.ts:339-352`) | M: stains, coffee rings, eraser smudges on the desk | S |
| 48 | Particles | many small sprites with simple motion (dust, sparks, smoke) | air, energy, scale | RT: GPU sprites; offline: more, with real depth sorting and motion blur | yes: `dotsOf` (`scenes/_pa.ts:155`), `dust3D` (`scenes/_kc-look.ts:310`), capped by R25 | H within the cap | - |

## 2. Top 10 for Pay Attention, ranked

Ranking = fit to treatment 3 and R31, times visibility at 4K, divided by cost. Each rule-tied.

| rank | pick | why it ranks here | build sketch (kit function, layer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Thin-lens depth of field by sub-frame aperture jitter** (with bokeh discs and focus pulls) | turns attention into optics: the sung word sharp, the rest of the one space soft; exact, with no screen-space halos, because every sub-frame is a real pinhole view | `_pa.ts persp()`: add `ap` (aperture, world units) and `zf` (focus distance); per sub-frame take an aperture point (ax, ay) on a disc from a low-discrepancy sequence; `x = cx + f*((X - cam.x - ax)/dz + ax/zf)` (same for y). Everything at dz = zf stays put; the rest smears into a circle of confusion `f*ap*|1/dz - 1/zf|`, and point lights become bokeh discs for free. Risks: a held frame stops at 12 sub-frames (ENGINE.md row "adaptive motion blur"), too few for smooth bokeh, so DOF shots need `--min-samples` 36+; whether `Frame` exposes the sub-frame index is unverified (needed to index the sequence); text drawn in screen space bypasses `persp` and stays sharp, which is the correct behaviour only for the meter (rule 4) |
| 2 | **Area-light soft shadows by sub-frame light jitter** | replaces the 2-step blur fake (`_pa.ts:270-277`) with true penumbrae: hard at contact, wider with distance, and shaped by the lamp's real size; also gives soft rims | `lightPass()`: per sub-frame offset each `Light`'s x, y inside a disc of radius `size` before building its shadow, and drop the blur steps; the accumulator averages them. `lightPass` already runs every sub-frame, so the code cost is small; render cost rises because noisy penumbrae need more sub-frames to converge (prior: 36-108 on lit plates) |
| 3 | **Tonemap with a toe + an exposure curve (eye adaptation as a function of t)** | the treatment's exposure ladder (3.1) and value bar (3.2) become one dial per section; a toe gives the true black and crushed darks it asks for | `engine/post.ts` `SHOULDER_GLSL`: add a toe (an AgX-style or Hable-style curve) behind a `toe` param; scenes return `exposure` in post overrides from a `keys()` curve over song time, eased over 0.5-1.5 s when the camera moves from dark into the lamp. Measure p1/p50/p99 per still as the treatment already requires |
| 4 | **Bounce light from lit surfaces** (one-bounce GI) | fills the true-black side with a faint lift from the page or desk the lamp hits, the strongest photoreal cue; every bounce has a visible source, so rule 5 holds | `lightPass`: after the key, find bright receiver regions (lit paper, the desk) and add 1-3 extra `Light`s at their centroids, intensity 3-8 % of the key, `size` = the patch size (so their shadows are very soft), no `rim`. Pure function of the plate, so every view agrees (rule 2) |
| 5 | **Planar reflections with Fresnel and roughness blur** | rule 2 defines reflections as the same place mirrored; a glossy desk or the laptop glass reflecting the lamp and the words proves the one space every time the camera moves | a kit `mirror(plane, rough)` in `_pa.ts`: redraw the plate's world items through `persp` with the camera mirrored across the plane, into a separate `Layer2D`; blur by `rough`, fade by Fresnel (stronger at grazing view), composite under the surface. Reflected text stays reversed (R31 worked example) |
| 6 | **View-dependent specular glints** | a highlight that slides across glass and glossy paper as the camera moves is the cheapest proof of 3D in a 2.5D engine | extend the rim step (`_pa.ts:322-331`): for surfaces with a known plane, place the highlight where the light's mirror image through the plane projects (half-vector), size by roughness; a moving camera moves the glint, a moving lamp moves it the other way |
| 7 | **One shared aerial-perspective rule** | ad hoc fog per plate (`late.ts:444`) risks two views of one place fading differently (rule 2) | a kit `air(dz)` in `_pa.ts` with section presets (clean air in verse, chalk haze in class); every plate's fog and contrast falloff calls it; fade toward ink (greyscale) |
| 8 | **Motivated volumetric haze with shafts through letters** | when the air is visibly dusty (chalk, steam), the lamp's cone and the letter-shaped shafts behind words are the treatment's "words cast shadows" pushed into the air | `lightPass`: radially blur the occluder mask `C` toward each light (Mitchell's 64-sample screen-space march; offline: 128+), multiply by `air(dz)` density and the light's falloff, add to the glow layer. Only where the plate shows the dust as particles too (rule 5: the haze needs a cause on screen) |
| 9 | **Raking-light paper relief** (bump map) | 4K detail that obeys the key light: fibre and impact-print relief lit from the lamp's side appears and disappears as the lamp ignites or moves | GLSL `fbm` (`engine/glsl/common.ts`) height field on paper receivers, shaded by the screen-space direction to the key in the light pass (`n dot l`); amplitude tiny (1-3 % value) so the clarity gate never reads it as texture through a word |
| 10 | **Shutter angle ladder per shot** | motion blur grammar for free: 180 degrees standard, 270-360 in the flights, 45-90 on the slams so stamps hit crisp | `TimelineEntry` or post override: per-entry `shutter`; the engine already takes one (`--shutter`, `still(t, samples, shutter)`); per-entry wiring is the build (prior: small) |

## 3. Traps (read as "video game" or "glitch", break R31)

| trap | what the viewer catches | rule broken |
|---|---|---|
| screen-space DOF halos | a sharp foreground word wrapped in a blurry fringe of the background, or background bleeding over it | 2, 6; avoided by lens jitter (#1) |
| blurring the sung word | the hero word out of focus on its lit frame | 6 and the clarity gate; focus pulls land on the word by its onset |
| too few sub-frames | stacked copies of a moving word, stepped bokeh, banded penumbrae | treatment 2.2 bans stacked-copy blur on text; raise `--min-samples` on these shots |
| AO halos in open air | dark rings round words that touch nothing | 5: a shadow with no cause; scale AO by height above the surface |
| shadow detached from contact ("peter-panning") | a shadow that starts a gap away from its object, so it floats | 1, 5 |
| light leaking | a light reaching through a wall or a closed laptop | 5 ("lights things it cannot reach") |
| god rays without a visible occluder or air | beams from nowhere | 5 and treatment 2.2 (beams as decoration) |
| bloom on non-sources | paper or type glowing | treatment 3.3; keep threshold above paper |
| lens flare, constant CA | the "video game camera" signature | treatment 2.4 |
| SSR edge fade | a reflection that vanishes as its object leaves the frame | 1, 2 |
| LOD or particle pop | detail that appears in one frame | 1 |
| auto-exposure pumping | brightness breathing on every beat with no light changing | 5: exposure moves only when a light ignites or the camera changes room |
| sharpening rings | white halos round dark type | 6 |
| full PBR + HDRI sets | the Blender or game-engine look | treatment 2.3 ban; keep greyscale type-and-paper materials |
| TAA-style history reuse | ghost trails behind moving words | breaks pure-function-of-t frames (ENGINE.md limits) and reads as smear |
| frozen grain | a static noise pattern reads as a dirty screen | none today (grain is seeded per frame); keep it so |
