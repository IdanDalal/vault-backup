# Tutoring — Session Skeleton (v1)

Steady-state session structure for all students: [[tutoring-sisters-9-12]], [[tutoring-cousin-son-9]], and [[tutoring-a-first-session|A]] (paused). Session **one** is different — it follows the first-contact plan (treasure hunt, baseline; see the per-student notes). The skeleton takes over from session two onward.

Decided 2026-07-13, brainstormed from solveeverything.org's Learning-Gain-per-Hour and retention-floor ideas. **Adopted:** baseline before session one, retention floors as a spacing ladder, n=1 method experiments via logging, parental transparency as product. **Rejected:** always-on AI tutoring (conflicts with AI-restraint principle), visible metrics/dashboards around the kid (conflicts with no-scorekeeping).

## The six blocks (~45 min)

| # | Block | Time | What happens | Data it emits |
|---|-------|------|--------------|---------------|
| 1 | Hello | 3 min | Warm way in, in English | — |
| 2 | Retrieval warm-up | 8–10 min | Game-format recall of items due on the ladder; immediate feedback on every attempt | hit/miss per item |
| 3 | New material | 12 min | One chunk: 5–7 workhorse words or one pattern + one wow-word; TPR-style — physical, visual, heavy repetition | items introduced + method used |
| 4 | Movement game | 8 min | Physical game mixing new items with old — doubles as the concentration break | informal retrieval signal |
| 5 | Story time | 10 min | Story recycling today's + previous vocab; student shifts from listener to co-teller over months | items produced unprompted |
| 6 | Goodbye | 3 min | Good way out + one-line "what I can say now" moment | seed for parent note |

Rules of the skeleton:

- **Rituals emerge, not installed.** Blocks 1 and 6 start as "a warm way in, a good way out"; whatever naturally recurs gets promoted to ritual around week three. (Per the transcripts principle: rhythm over formal ritual.)
- **The grid loses to the kid.** Block times are defaults. The day she's obsessed with something, follow it — and log what actually happened.
- **Block 2 feedback is immediate**, per the retrieval-practice research; misses are never dwelt on, the item just drops a rung.
- **Retention ladder (spacing):**
    - 2x/week: next session (3–4 days) → ~2 weeks → ~1 month → ~3 months
    - 1x/week: 1 week → 1 month → 3 months (warm-up gets the full 10 min; the parent note covers the mid-week gap — see below)
    - A missed item drops back one rung. Precision here buys little (expanding-vs-equal research is unsettled); simplicity wins.
- **Throughput:** ~10–14 new words/week at 2x, ~5–7 at 1x.

## Transition to story-spine (v2)

At **~100–150 words**, the story swallows blocks 3–4 (TPRS-proper): retrieval = retelling last session's story, new vocab introduced as the evolving story needs it, games act out scenes. Expected at ~2–2.5 months (2x/week) or ~4–5 months (1x/week). The 60/180-day capstones are block 5 with the audience changed to the parents.

## Parent note (part of the product)

Three jobs: (1) make invisible progress legible — the cumulative can-do ledger is the receipt for a free service; (2) carry between-session retrieval — structurally required at 1x/week, where the first recall otherwise lands a week out on the steepest part of the forgetting curve; (3) manage expectations — milestone arc pre-sells the story capstones.

WhatsApp format, filled from the session log in ≤3 min:

> 🌟 **Today's win** — one specific human moment
> 🆕 **New this session** — the words/pattern + running total
> 🎁 **She can teach you** — scripted 2-min prompt where the kid teaches the parent (retrieval-by-teaching: high effort for her, delight for them, zero homework dynamic)
> 🔭 **Coming up** — occasional; next milestone or capstone date

Cadence (decided 2026-07-13):

- **2x/week:** micro-note after each session (win + teach-prompt only); full ledger/milestone note weekly.
- **1x/week:** full note per session, sent a day or two **after** the session so the teach-prompt fires mid-gap.

Constraints: **positive-only** — struggles live in the private log, never the note (a report card breaks no-scorekeeping and risks parents relaying pressure). Sisters get **separate ledgers** even if taught together — a shared note invites comparison, which is scorekeeping through the side door.

## Research anchors

- Spaced retrieval + immediate feedback beats re-exposure for primary-age vocabulary; week-later recall ≈ 5-min recall: [primary-school study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259679706_The_Effect_of_Retrieval_Practice_in_Primary_School_Vocabulary_Learning), [multi-study review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8126157/), [Nature Reviews overview](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00089-1)
- Expanding vs equal-interval spacing unsettled → don't tune the ladder: [Karpicke & Roediger 2007](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2007/2007_Karpicke_Roediger_JEPLMC.pdf)
- Routine is load-bearing for young learners: [British Council](https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/managing-lesson/articles/managing-very-young-learners)
- TPR/TPRS for young beginners (oral skills, vocab): [TPRS young learners](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334794903_Using_Teaching_Proficiency_Through_Reading_and_Storytelling_TPRS_in_Teaching_English_for_Young_Learners), [third-grader vocab study](https://www.jlls.org/index.php/jlls/article/view/2237)
- 45–60 min viable at this age with activity rotation; mid-session movement restores concentration: [session length](https://tutoring.k12.com/resources/parent-guides/formats-and-scheduling/30-vs-60-minute-sessions/are-30-or-60-minute-tutoring-sessions-better-for-elementary-kids/), [physical-break study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10668119/)
- Rejected Option C (short frequent sessions, [Stanford short-burst](https://ed.stanford.edu/news/short-bursts-tutoring-improves-young-readers-skills-only-minutes-day-stanford-study-finds)) on parent optics: reads against Israel's 45-min school standard; parents value the time off.
