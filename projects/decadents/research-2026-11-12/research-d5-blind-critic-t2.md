Cast-wide note: no image is funny by itself. The void reads as comic only where the pose is mundane (41: a man with no head drinking coffee). Stern poses (43, 44) and realism (50) push it to threat.

## 5. Ranking of the six designs

1. **Poster (43/44)** - the only design clean in both takes; highest craft; hollow collar gives a true void; shape (dome, black, collar) is repeatable. Risks: menacing tone, German-leaning helmet.
2. **Ink cartoon (41/42)** - most authentic 1940s artefact and the most humane tone; one take near clean. Fix list: remove the white neck band, widen the black to the collar edges, glove both hands.
3. **Comic void (39/40)** - convincing print and a ready balloon for jokes; both takes leak ear and neck in the same place, so the flaw is systematic. The white shirt under the oval builds a neck.
4. **Censor photo (49/50)** - the strongest joke on paper (the censor did it), unreliable in practice: one take uncovered, the other mask-like with a typo. Realism turns it grim.
5. **Caption box (47/48)** - sound drawing, zero for two on the brief; the box covers the torso and the face stays.
6. **Cel (45/46)** - zero for two on the brief, faces fully drawn, 1990s TV look, French helmet.
