# DECADEnts round 2 - blind critic review (32 renders + 4 accepted PNGs)

Method: every image opened and viewed (32 JPEGs in `renders-r2\`, 4 PNGs in `vault\projects\decadents\renders\`). No code, prompts or seeds seen. Scores 1 to 5. Sizes quoted as "% of frame" are my estimates by eye, unmeasured.

## 0. The answer in 76 words

Most of these show a person wearing or hiding behind something, and you can still see the person: necks, ears, eyes, hair, bare arms. The head object is usually too small, drawn in a different style from the body, or lit by light that does not exist in the room. The old decades look like clean modern digital photos with a filter. And nothing funny is happening: every character just stands in the middle and poses.

The exact cues that read as machine-made, in order of how loudly they read:
1. Two rendering styles in one frame (rank 6). A photograph does not contain a flat black polygon or a vector triangle.
2. A lit neck directly under a black face (09 to 12), and chrome that mirrors a room that is absent (13 to 16).
3. Cracks drawn as even-width lines on a perfect surface (01 to 04).
4. Objects that pass through each other: straw-holder in the drink, cigar out of the phone.
5. Noise-free smoothness in images that claim to be 1920s to 1940s film.
6. Lettering that almost spells words (19, 20).

Also: the 32 images are 16 compositions rendered twice. Within a pair, consistency is high. Between pairs of the same character, it is low.

What sets the three accepted designs apart:
1. **The head is one solid manufactured object, head-sized or larger, that replaces the head.** The CRT is about 1.5 shoulder-widths of presence and fills roughly 35 to 40% of frame height. The toaster fills about 20%, the lava lamp about 8% of frame width, the disco ball about 15% of height.
2. The object has a face plane (screen, visor) that can carry expression while the cast stays faceless. The crying emoji sits on a visor, which is a display, so it stays an object.
3. One light explains everything. On-camera flash: hot spot in the glass, hard shadow on the panelling, same light on denim. Head and body sit in one photograph.
4. The decade's look is one the model draws natively. 1990s compact-camera flash snapshot and 2020s clean digital are both heavily photographed. 1920s nitrate stills and 1930s large-format documentary come out faked.
5. Medium shot, so the head wins at thumbnail size.
6. Locked design: same jacket, shirt and room across all three CRT frames.
7. The child stacks four readable jokes (juice boxes in an ammo vest, crying visor, dinosaur patch, dropped tablet) and has deliberate squat proportions, so it reads as a designed character. No r2 image has one such joke.

## 5. What would get the three weakest approved

**Yuppie (1980s)**
1. Replace the floating shapes with one physical object. Candidates that read 80s at a glance: the brick phone itself as the head, a Rubik's cube, a boombox, a Memphis-style table lamp or clock (a real 3D object in those colours).
2. Collar closes on the object's base; no gap, no stump.
3. Location becomes a photographable 80s place (office with venetian blinds and a beige computer, a car with a car phone). Memphis pattern moves to the tie or the wallpaper.
4. Direct flash or hard office fluorescents, 35 mm colour negative look.
5. Phone specified as a large grey handset with a thick antenna, held alone; cigar in the other hand or in an ashtray.

**Hippie Grandma (1960s)**
1. Lamp scaled up until the glass is head-width and the metal base is as wide as the neck opening, seated inside a high collar or a knitted cowl. No visible stand, no bust.
2. A living posture: seated in a rocking chair, needles in gloved hands, half-finished scarf, weight in the body. Hands closer than the elbows to break the mannequin splay.
3. Cardigan, long skirt, thick stockings, slippers in every frame. Drop the caftan variant.
4. Plain or softly patterned wall. No posters with faces or lettering.
5. Lamp glow should tint the collar and shoulders orange, so head and body share light.
6. A joke: the scarf she knits is lava-coloured, or the yarn runs up into the lamp.

**Gatsby (1920s)**
1. The head stops being a mask. Options: a featureless glazed porcelain egg or vase, a cocktail shaker, a gramophone horn, a champagne coupe. If porcelain stays: blank ovoid, no eye holes, no nose, no lips.
2. Damage rendered as material: one missing shard showing dark hollow inside, crazing as a fine uneven web, chipped rim. Lines of even width fail.
3. One figure per frame, always.
4. Holder and glass in separate hands, or the holder clearly pinched between two fingers above the glass.
5. Period photograph spec: soft uncoated lens, visible grain, glow around highlights, deep staged focus, theatrical key light with a hard shadow. Then grain added in post if the model stays clean.
6. Bow tie and wing collar; the collar hides the join.

Fourth-weakest, flagged because its fault sits in bin (b): **the Soldier needs an object.** A helmet tipped down over an empty collar, a helmet with a tall object inside, a ration tin, a field radio. "Shadow" cannot be held constant across a series.
