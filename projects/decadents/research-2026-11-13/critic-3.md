# Blind scoring, second set - critic 3

40 pictures judged by eye against `blind2/briefs.md`. Totals: yes 28, partly 12, no 0. Per-picture detail is in `critic-3.json`. Items marked "probable" are small patches I could not resolve at this size.

## 1930s-B
- Best: `q` has dark mirrored eyepieces reflecting sky and horizon with nothing behind them. `p` is the better photograph (look 5) but fails the eyepiece detail.
- Common failure: clear eyepieces. In `p` a pale surface with an eyelid line shows through both lenses, so the mask reads as worn by a person.
- Brief change: say "opaque black glass, like welding goggles" and drop "tinted". Add "adult build" since `q` looks child-sized, and name the print as "grey silver gelatin, no sepia".

## 1940s-A
- Best: `p` for the shadow itself (reads as real darkness under a real brim, running into the collar). `r` and `q` follow the instruction too.
- Common failure: the void is a hard-edged black oval with a chin, so it reads as a black mask (`q`, `r`). Helmets drift to riveted kettle and French crested shapes. `p` lost the flag pin and has a red and green duotone cast.
- Brief change: name the helmet ("US M1 pot helmet, olive drab") and describe the void as "darkness that fills the helmet and the collar opening, no outline of a chin".

## 1940s-B
- Best: `q`, then `p`. Backlight explains the black, and the void runs from brim into the open collar with no skin.
- Common failure: `r` blacks out only the face and leaves a fully lit bare neck, jaw side and ear area. `p` and `q` look like modern golden-hour digital shots with crested safety helmets.
- Brief change: add "collar buttoned to the top, scarf or shadow fills the neck" and name the helmet. Replace "lens flare" with "hard sun, deep shadows", since the flare pulls the modern cinematic look.

## 1950s-A
- Best: `p`. Toaster, grey fedora, dark gap at the collar, white glove, pastel ad light.
- Common failure: toaster anatomy drifts. `p` has slots on the side face; `q` has two levers and two dials that form an accidental face, plus a teal modern grade and a lampshade stacked behind the hat.
- Brief change: add "slots on top under the hat, plain mirrored sides, no dials on the front" and "lamp to one side of the figure".

## 1950s-B
- Best: `q`. Right scale, real room reflections, dark gap at the collar, one light. Look 5.
- Common failure: scale and seating of the toaster. `p` is a tiny toaster perched on the shoulders with a cartoonish reflection. `r` sits like a helmet with a probable brown neck patch under its base.
- Brief change: state the size ("toaster as wide as the shoulders' inner span, base resting on the shirt collar, collar closed around it"). Remove "like a backyard snapshot", which contradicts "advertising photograph".

## 1960s-A
- Best: `r`, then `q`. Lamp base sits inside the buttoned collar, gloves, one warm window light.
- Common failure: small ones only. `p` is framed full-figure with a thin lamp and a pale shape at the hem that may be a toe. `q` has clasped hands with no knitting.
- Brief change: this wording works; keep "buttoned to the top" as the pattern for other briefs. Add "knitting needles and yarn visible in the gloved hands" and "feet hidden under the skirt".

## 1960s-B
- Best: `q` for the mount (flared base replaces the neck). `p` also mounts the lamp on the shoulders.
- Common failure: all three show a deep V-neck with pale chest and bare arms between short sleeves and gloves. `r` is a shop dummy with a lamp on its neck stump. Posters in `q` and `r` carry readable faces.
- Brief change: remove "mannequin", which produces dress-form skin. Replace "kaftan" with "high-necked long-sleeved paisley tunic closed at the throat". Ask for "abstract psychedelic posters, patterns only, no people or faces".

## 1970s-A
- Best: `p`. Fully covered, high sequined collar, and the most believable period print (look 5).
- Common failure: cropped jumpsuit legs put the cover job on the socks. `r` shows a probable tan band of shin above each skate. `q` is clean modern digital.
- Brief change: say "full-length jumpsuit to the ankle" or "knee-high white tube socks meeting the jumpsuit hem". Add "faded drugstore print, on-camera flash" to hold the look.

## 1970s-B
- Best: `r`. Convincing flash snapshot (look 5), no skin anywhere. `p` and `q` also follow.
- Common failure: collar detail varies (beaded in `p`, plain satin in `q`, short gold band in `r`). `r` has a wire from the ball to the ceiling. Tube socks are missing in `p`. Background people appear in `p` and `r`.
- Brief change: add "no wire or chain above the ball" and "empty rink" if the figure must be alone. "Flash snapshot" is the phrase that earns the period look.

## 1980s-A
- Best: none follow fully. `r` is closest because its shapes have some depth, but a pink neck form fills the collar.
- Common failure: flat 2D shapes pasted over a real head, with neck, jaw, ear sliver (`p`) and bare wrist (`q`) showing. The phone turns into a modern smartphone with an Apple-style camera bump (`p`), a thin handset (`q`) or a lump of brick wall (`r`).
- Brief change: "brick cellphone" is read literally; write "1980s mobile phone the size of a brick, grey plastic, long antenna". Describe the head as "solid painted wooden blocks" and the collar as "empty, black inside, nothing in it".

## 1980s-B
- Best: `p`. Solid 3D shapes over an empty black collar, no skin. `r` also has an empty collar but flat paper shapes and a wrist sliver.
- Common failure: `q` shows a complete headless human neck. Shapes in `q` and `r` are flat clip art. Phones are wrong in all three (undersized, 2000s candybar, literal brick). `p` reads as a modern 3D render.
- Brief change: same phone and "solid blocks" wording as 1980s-A. The blazer plus turtleneck layer in `p` hid the neck, so keep a high inner collar. Add "glove tucked under the cuff".

## 2000s-A
- Best: `r` for the head (abstract mosaic, no face, hood up, no skin). `p` has the only true 2004 compact-camera body and setting.
- Common failure: the cube is a sharp, flat-lit insert that ignores the scene light in all three and resembles a Minecraft block. `q` carries a skin-toned mosaic with eye and mouth patches, so it reads as a game character head. `q` and `r` have no flash.
- Brief change: avoid "pixelated cube", which pulls Minecraft. Try "a cardboard box painted with blurry low-resolution landscape texture, lit by the flash". State "no face pattern, no skin colours on the box".

## 2010s-A
- Best: `p` and `q`. Glossy ball, sunglasses, wide smile, black collar, gloves and socks cover everything, light matches the room.
- Common failure: `r` has a skin-toned neck under the ball and bare ankles above the sneakers. `p` and `q` are a little too polished for a 2015 phone.
- Brief change: add "black turtleneck, black socks covering the ankles". The wording otherwise works.

## 2010s-B
- Best: `q`, then `r`. A real glossy ball in the scene light, fully covered.
- Common failure: no mirror in `q` or `r`, and neither ball has a mouth. `p` is a flat emoji sticker in Apple's artwork style over a real head, with a neck sliver and probably bare fingers; it is also the only true mirror selfie.
- Brief change: "mirror selfie" plus "emoji" triggers the sticker-over-face habit. Write "a large inflatable yellow ball with a painted smile" and "seen reflected in a wall mirror, mirror frame visible". Copy the wide smile line from 2010s-A.

## Across the set
- Briefs that name the garment closing the neck ("buttoned to the top", "high collar", turtleneck) leak least. Briefs with open collars, kaftans or cropped legs leak most.
- Flat graphic heads (Memphis shapes, pixel cube, emoji) lose the look score every time; solid physical objects (toaster, lamp, disco ball, gas mask) hold one light with the body.
- Real-company likenesses seen: Minecraft-style block (2000s-A, all three, strongest in `q`), Apple emoji artwork (2010s-B `p`), Apple-style camera bump (1980s-A `p`).
