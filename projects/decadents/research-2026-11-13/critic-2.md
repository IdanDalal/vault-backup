# Critic 2 - blind scoring, briefs 1960s to 2010s

34 pictures judged by eye. Totals: 14 yes, 17 partly, 3 no. Scores per picture: `critic-2.json`.

Conventions used in the JSON:
- `followed` judges only the head details the brief states. A neck under an emoji ball is reported in `leak` and does not lower `followed`, because that brief states no neck detail.
- `bg_faces` is true in three 1960s-B rows with `figures` 0: the faces are poster art, no background people.
- Uncertain calls are marked "uncertain" or "read as" in `leak`.

## 1960s-A (4 pictures: 1 yes, 3 partly)
1. Best: `1960s-A_d` - the metal base sits straight on the buttoned collar, no neck, and the figure knits. Its look is too clean for the decade (3 of 5).
2. Common failure: the lamp stands on a visible neck. `_a` and `_c` show a skin-coloured neck and chest in the neckline; `_b` shows a cream dress-form stub and looks like a modern phone photo (2 of 5).
3. Brief change: "mannequin figure" invites a dummy neck and bust. Drop "mannequin", ask for a cardigan with a high closed collar that the lamp base rests on, and state the gloved hands hold knitting needles (two of four have idle hands on the floor).

## 1960s-B (4 pictures: 1 yes, 3 partly)
1. Best: `1960s-B_d` - a ribbed metal base replaces the neck, sleeves reach the wrists. `1960s-B_b` has the most convincing film look but stands the lamp on a dummy neck base.
2. Common failure: a V-neck kaftan exposes a chest under the lamp (`_a`, `_b`, `_c`), and in `_a` and `_c` the sleeves end at the elbow, so both forearms are bare. In `_b` and `_d` the lamp is too small to read as a head.
3. Brief change: ask for a high round neckline, state "sleeves to the gloves", and give the lamp a size ("as large as a head"). Posters bring faces into frame (`_a`, `_c`, `_d`) and one poster repeats the lava lamp (`_c`): ask for abstract pattern posters with no faces or figures.

## 1970s-A (4 pictures: 2 yes, 2 partly)
1. Best: `1970s-A_d` (suit meets ribbed socks, no gap) and `1970s-A_a` (knee socks, sequined neck). Both hold head and body in one light.
2. Common failure: a cropped jumpsuit leaves the lower leg open. `_b` shows bare shins above ankle socks; `_c` shows smooth beige calves that read as bare, in the most convincing 1970s print of the set (5 of 5).
3. Brief change: replace "tube socks" with "white knee-high tube socks pulled over the jumpsuit legs", or ask for a full-length jumpsuit to the skates.

## 1970s-B (4 pictures: 4 yes)
1. Best: `1970s-B_b` and `1970s-B_d` - ball on a sequin collar, mid-glide, convincing faded prints with motion blur (5 of 5 each). No skin leak in any of the four.
2. Common failure: small ones only. `_a` and `_c` keep a hanging string from the ball to the ceiling, which lets the ball read as a prop in front of a hidden head; `_b` and `_d` have a ball a little small for a head; `_a` and `_d` include a blurred background skater with no face.
3. Brief change: add "no string or chain on the ball" and "empty rink". This brief is the strongest of my nine and needs nothing else.

## 1980s-A (4 pictures: 2 partly, 2 no)
1. Best: `1980s-A_a` - no face, shapes hover over the collar. It still shows a band of neck skin at the collar base and a bare wrist.
2. Common failure: flat vector stickers pasted over a real man. `_c` and `_d` show mouth, chin, neck and hair; `_b` shows neck. All four score 2 of 5 for look because the shapes share no light with the photograph. No attempt shows a brick cellphone: the phones are 2000s handsets or a small silver box.
3. Brief change: describe the shapes as physical objects ("painted wooden and foam shapes hanging on thin wires") and the collar as "an empty shirt collar, dark inside, standing open with nothing in it". Ask for "a large 1980s brick mobile phone with a long antenna". Add a buttoned cuff tucked into the glove, since every attempt shows wrist skin.

## 1980s-B (4 pictures: 2 yes, 1 partly, 1 no)
1. Best: `1980s-B_b` - empty collar with a dark void, shapes that read as paper cut-outs casting shadows, no skin. `1980s-B_c` also has an empty collar but is a plain cut-out collage (1 of 5) with a bare wrist.
2. Common failure: `_a` shows a man's lower face, `_d` a bare neck stump with Adam's apple. No attempt looks like a 1980s photograph; the best reads as a 2000s digital snapshot.
3. Brief change: "black and white grid cube" comes out as a Rubik's Cube shaped puzzle in all four pictures, a real toy brand's design. Use "a flat checkerboard square" or "a black cube with thin white grid lines, no stickers". Same physical-object wording as 1980s-A.

## 2000s-A (2 pictures: 2 yes)
1. Both are a cube with pixel texture, no monitor, no character, and no face or neck in view. `2000s-A_a` hides the wrists better.
2. Common failure: the cube is a sharp computer graphic pasted on a convincing 2004 flash photograph (2 of 5 each), and both textures resemble Minecraft blocks (an ore block in `_a`, a gravel-topped block in `_b`). `_a` seats the figure on a car bonnet.
3. Brief change: "pixelated cube" pulls a 2010s game franchise's block design. Ask for "a cardboard box wrapped in a printed low-resolution texture of grey stone and rust, 1998 console style, lit by the flash". Say "the hoodie's hood" to stop the car reading.

## 2010s-A (4 pictures: 2 yes, 2 partly)
1. Best: `2010s-A_c` and `2010s-A_d` - a physical glossy ball with real sunglasses in the room's light, generic design (4 of 5 each). Both show a skin-toned neck under the ball and bare ankles, and the smile is a small cut-out, narrower than the "wide smile" asked for.
2. Common failure: `_a` and `_b` paste a flat emoji sticker in the look of Apple's emoji set over a real head; `_a` leaks neck, hair and a second person in a doorway. Bare ankles show in all four.
3. Brief change: describe the object, drop the word emoji: "a large glossy yellow plastic sphere with a painted wide smile, wearing real black sunglasses, resting on the jacket collar". Add "high black socks" or "leggings tucked into the sneakers", and a scarf or turtleneck under the ball.

## 2010s-B (4 pictures: 4 partly)

2. Common failure: human parts around the sticker - long hair in `_b`, bare fingers from fingerless gloves in `_c`, neck skin in `_a` and `_d`. Phones copy real iPhone designs: an Apple logo in `_c`, late-model camera blocks in `_a` and `_d`, wrong for 2015.
3. Brief change: the mirror-selfie framing pulls the "sticker over my face" habit of real social posts. Use the physical-sphere wording from 2010s-A, keep the phone on the stick away from the head, and add "plain phone with no logo, full-finger gloves".
