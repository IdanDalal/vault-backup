# Critic 1 - blind verdicts, briefs 1920s to 1950s

40 pictures judged. Followed: 20 yes, 17 partly, 3 no. bg_faces is true only where a background person's features are readable. Uncertain leaks are marked "not certain" in the JSON.

## 1920s-A
- Best: 1920s-A_d is the only full pass - smooth white porcelain, gold glaze cracks, no mouth, no eyes. 1920s-A_f and 1920s-A_c have the strongest Deco and crack detail but both carry lips.
- Common failure: the word "mask" produces a face mask with eye holes and sculpted lips (a, c, e, f), sometimes worn on a visible head (ear in a, hair rim and an eye-like shape in e). The two pictures with the cleanest heads (b, d) have the weakest period look: sepia, modern blur.
- Brief change: say "a smooth blank porcelain head with no eye holes, no lips, no ears" and drop "mask". Ask for neutral black and white, since the sepia attempts look modern. The cigarette holder keeps turning into a stick in the glass; give it its own hand or cut it.

## 1920s-B
- Best: 1920s-B_c is the only full pass - blank cracked head, nose bump only, coupe and cigarette in one grip. 1920s-B_a is close but has a sculpted ear, a faint mouth and a wrist gap.
- Common failure: two opposite ones. b and f look like real 1920s stills but the head is a porcelain doll with a painted eye, brow, lips and an ear. d and e ignore "one figure" (three and two masked people) and put masks over heads with hair showing.
- Brief change: "seated at a table in a speakeasy" invites companions, so state "alone, empty chairs". Add "no painted features, no eye, no ear". The profile view (f) exposes the ear; ask for a frontal or three-quarter front view.

## 1930s-A
- Best: 1930s-A_c - canvas hood, mirrored eyepieces reflecting the street, real documentary look. 1930s-A_f also correct at the head. 1930s-A_a and 1930s-A_d are correct heads with a modern sepia render look.
- Common failure: b and e have clear glass and show human eyes with brows, a hard fail. In the passing pictures the queue brings readable faces into frame (c, f) and f's crowd wears later-century clothing.
- Brief change: the eyepiece wording works in 4 of 6; add "opaque black glass" as a second lock. If the cast rule covers everyone in frame, write "the queue seen from behind". Ask for "canvas hood" to stop the smooth rubber masks.

## 1930s-B
- Best: 1930s-B_a and 1930s-B_d are the most convincing photographs of all 40, with nothing behind the eyepieces. 1930s-B_b has the best mirrored eyepieces (a horizon in each) but a modern render look.
- Common failure: c and e have pale see-through eyepieces, and e shows a brow and eye shape through the right one. a and d each have one pale and one dark eyepiece.
- Brief change: "reflecting the sky" makes the glass pale and then transparent. Use "black mirrored glass, both the same". d crops the hands; ask for gloved hands in frame if they matter.

## 1940s-A
- Best: 1940s-A_c (helmet down on the collar, figure turned away, no flesh) and 1940s-A_d (solid black under the brim running into the collar). Both hide the neck, which is why they pass.
- Common failure: a and b paint a flat black oval over the face only and leave ears, neck, open-collar chest and one wrist lit. It looks pasted on. Three helmets across the 1940s set flare like German WW2 helmets on a US-style uniform.
- Brief change: the shadow has to cover the neck too, or the collar has to hide it - "collar turned up and closed, scarf at the throat, black shadow from brim to collar". Name the helmet "US M1 helmet". d's colour is a red and green two-tone cast; drop "slight colour shift" from the prompt.

## 1940s-B
- Best: 1940s-B_d - backlit, head bowed, face in near-black, fully convincing photograph. It still shows a human nose, lips and chin as an outline, so it is a partly.
- Common failure: no picture passes. a is a readable human profile in dim light. b and c use the pasted black patch with a sunlit neck and ear. Backlight plus a side view always draws a human profile.
- Brief change: backlight cannot hide a profile contour, so demand a frontal view: "facing the camera, sun directly behind, the whole head and neck a single black shape under the helmet". Add the closed collar. Otherwise this brief should be redesigned, since shadow alone does not replace a head.

## 1950s-A
- Best: 1950s-A_c - head-sized toaster, hat, single knob, black gap for the neck, warm colour. 1950s-A_a and 1950s-A_d are also clean. All five follow the head instruction.
- Common failure: two round knobs under two slots or levers read as eyes and brows (b, d, e), so the toaster becomes a face. Small flesh slivers at collar (b) and wrists (a). The toaster is smaller than a head in a and b.
- Brief change: specify "one side lever, no round knobs on the front" to kill the face read, "head-sized", and "gloved hands resting in view". The fedora instruction works every time.

## 1950s-B
- Best: 1950s-B_b - large chrome toaster reflecting the room, both white gloves visible, the best period colour of the brief. All four follow the head instruction.
- Common failure: neck-coloured slivers under the base (c, d), cropped hands with no clear gloves (c, d), a flesh-toned flap in place of the bow tie and a pink lump beside the toaster (a). Chrome reflections contain skin-toned shapes. Knobs again form eyes.
- Brief change: add "the toaster sits directly on the closed collar, no gap", "white gloves in view", and "reflection shows only the empty room". No company logos or franchise designs seen in any of the 40 pictures; the only real-world reference is the German helmet shape noted under 1940s-A.
