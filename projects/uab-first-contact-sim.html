Phone state at the door

  • Her-territory screenshots & clips saved locally — cued, tested, no buffering, no ads.
  • Notifications silenced; emergency alerts on. The phone appears only as a prop player.
  • No notes app open, ever, in front of her. The scoring sheet lives in your head until she's gone.

The bag (physical)

  • Drawing supplies · small TPR props · letter cards · the "secret message" sentence strip written with her name in it.
  • Domain load per student — the cousin's son gets his games' vocabulary; each sister gets her own world. 9 and 12 do not share a bag.
Rule zeroWhatever the parents told you about level — you heard it, you smiled, and everyone still starts at rung zero. Reports chose the props in this bag. Nothing else.

No rung — the lesson has already started

You sayHello, Noa! English at the door, warm and unremarkable — then straight into Hebrew small talk. No syllabus speech, no "so, we're going to learn English." You came to hang out.

Branches

10as planned

"Hello!" — echoed back, maybe shy, maybe grinning.

You do: nothing special. Sit down wherever she leads.

You note: greeting reflex exists — one A3 chunk already banked.

→ Beat 2, on schedule.

7wobble

Silent smile, half-hides behind the parent.

You do: slow everything down. Two minutes of Hebrew with the parent while she orbits. Zero English pressure.

You note: possible silent period — normal, plan for pointing/acting answers all session.

→ Beat 2, gently, via her stuff ("what's that poster?").

4off-script

"אמא מכריחה אותי" — mom is making me do this.

You do: drop the word "lesson" from your vocabulary permanently. "Good — me neither. I came to see your room / hear about Minecraft." Mean it.

You note: arrangement pressure. The only KPI today is that she asks when you're coming back.

→ Beat 2 disguised as pure snooping around her interests.

1chaos edge

Won't come out of her room.

You do: don't force it, don't negotiate through the door. Coffee with the parent, 10 relaxed minutes, leave warmly.

You note: environment intel from the parent chat (prep signal only!). Reschedule with a hook: "next time I'm bringing something for her."

→ Session one hasn't failed; it just hasn't started. Return to Claude anyway — this is data.

20:03–0:11

A0 — the treasure hunt

Track A · Rung 0 · no pass bar — this rung exists for rapport and the revelation

You say (Hebrew)מה אתה הכי אוהב לעשות? אם לא הייתי פה עכשיו — מה היית עושה? What do you love doing most? If I weren't here, what would you be doing? — then hunt the English already hiding inside the answer: pizza, OK, bye, internet, GG, craft, gol…

Branches

10as planned

Names a world instantly — Minecraft, drawing, football — and lights up talking about it.

You do: hunt inside it. "You already say craft. And OK. And gol. Who taught you English? Nobody? You stole it yourself." Watch the eyes widen.

You note: every owned word — this list is cohort S0. And the domain: it's your content mine for months.

→ Beat 3 if her domain is media/games; else Beat 4.

7wobble

"לא יודעת" — shrug. I don't know.

You do: hunt the room instead — phone, computer, pizza box, brand logos on her shirt. Or "what did you do yesterday?" Concrete beats abstract.

You note: shy or genuinely undecided — the room hunt reveals the same inventory, just slower.

→ Beat 4; keep probing for the domain all session, it'll surface.

4off-script

Answers, but the domain is English-free — playing with the cat, cooking with grandma.

You do: respect the domain (cat = cat! first inventory word from her own world), then widen to house objects and logos.

You note: low-exposure environment, whatever the parents claimed. Bag's media props may be dead weight today.

→ Beat 4; skip Beat 3 unless media surfaces later.

1chaos edge

Monosyllables. Arms crossed. The talking approach is dead on arrival.

You do: stop talking, start doing. Pull paper, start drawing something badly, wait. Kids can't resist correcting a bad drawing.

You note: verbal-first doesn't fit this kid — that's baseline data about learning style, not failure.

→ Ladder pauses. Rapport is the only rung. Beats 4–7 may wait for session two entirely.

~5 min

The It-Takes-Two probe

Conditional — runs the moment her media territory surfaces

The single highest-information probe in the session, because it separates the three cases that "he plays in English all day" collapses together: absorbed meaning, absorbed sounds, absorbed nothing. Your neighbor played an entire fully-voiced game and asked if there were more levels after the story's emphatic ending. Assume nothing.

You say (Hebrew)ספר לי מה קורה במשחק — מי הדמות? מה היא רוצה? Tell me the game's story — who's the character, what do they want? Then the cued screenshot: "what does it say here?" / "what did she just say?"

You're watching

plot detail = meaning absorbedconfidence vs accuracy gapdecoding vs guessing from icons

Branches

10stealth fluent

Tells the plot with detail AND reads the screenshot text, translating on the fly.

You do: hide your surprise; keep it a conversation about the game.

You note: expect a high climb. Touch A1–A3 briefly anyway (never skip rungs, just walk them fast) and budget real time for A4.

→ Beats 4–6 at double speed, then Beat 8 = keep climbing.

7meaning, no text

Rich plot summary in Hebrew — but the on-screen sentence is a wall.

You do: nothing visible. "So YOU'RE the one who has to tell me what happens next season."

You note: comprehension through audio/visuals, literacy gap likely — Track B just became the interesting track.

→ Beat 4 normally; give Beat 7 full attention.

4the IT2 case

Plays daily — but the "story" is "יורים באנשים" (you shoot people). No characters, no wants, no narrative.

You do: stay warm; the game is still your best content mine even if nothing came through it yet.

You note: exposure without absorption confirmed. Treat comprehension as near-zero regardless of hours logged. The parents' report is now formally retired.

→ Beat 4 with zero assumptions, exactly as the ladder intends.

1defenses up

"למה אתה בודק אותי?" — why are you testing me? (The 12-year-old, most likely.)

You do: drop the probe instantly. "Testing? I'm terrible at this game — show me how you play." Genuine interest only; the data arrives on its own while she shows off.

You note: assessment-radar is active — every remaining rung needs a thicker disguise.

→ Beats 4–6 dressed as games she runs, not games you run.

40:11–0:16

A1 — receptive (her body answers)

Track A · Rung 1 · clean pass: ≥7 of 8 instant

You sayJump! … Touch something red. … Give me the pencil. Simon Says / floor-is-lava skin. Eight commands. Deliver them deadpan, hands still — your gestures are an answer key she will read if you leak it.

Branches

10as planned

Seven or eight instant, giggling, wants harder ones.

You do: give her one absurd command as a reward ("touch your nose with the pencil!") and move on while it's fun.

You note: A1 clean; which fields were instant vs slow.

→ Beat 5.

7reading you

Four to six right — but her eyes are on your hands and face, mining hints.

You do: smile, hands behind back, re-run three commands. Count only the clean ones.

You note: strong pragmatic intelligence, thinner vocabulary than it first appears. Frontier is forming here.

→ One more same-rung probe, then Beat 5 or mark frontier.

4frontier found

One to three right; does random funny actions to keep the game alive.

You do: keep it a game — end on the two she DOES know, land the win loudly.

You note: A1 frontier. That's not bad news; that's the baseline doing its job.

→ Skip Beats 5–6, go to Beat 7 (literacy is orthogonal — always run it).

1shame flicker

Freezes on the first command. Face falls. She knows she doesn't know.

You do: flip roles that second: "Wait — YOU command ME." She orders in Hebrew, you act it out and echo the English word each time. She's laughing at you within a minute.

You note: A1 at zero, and — more important — shame is nearby. Every future miss needs a soft floor.

→ Beat 7 via the role-flip game; skip 5–6 today.

10as planned

Streams words you never hunted — school leftovers, YouTube absorbs, song lyrics.

You do: keep her going until she runs dry. Every word is S0 inventory.

You note: the real inventory (memorize it — write nothing). Domains that produce.

7the echo signal

Reproduces exactly the words from the treasure hunt, twenty minutes ago — and nothing else.

You do: celebrate it: she's teaching you words she "stole" an hour into knowing you.

You note: ⭐ short-loop retention is strong — the single best predictor for how her cohorts will behave on the ladder. Small inventory, fast learner. Great combination.

→ Beat 6, expectations calibrated.

4receptive > productive

Understood everything in Beat 4, but now: "אני יודעת מה זה, אני לא זוכרת איך אומרים" — I know what it is, I can't say it.

You do: no pushing. Offer first sounds ("ca—?") as a game, accept pointing.

You note: classic receptive-over-productive profile — a clean, common, useful baseline. Frontier between A1 and A2.

→ Beat 7; A3 probes only if energy allows.

1role refusal

Being "the teacher" mortifies her. Shuts down.

You do: hand the teacher role to a stuffed animal, or start "forgetting" words yourself — "the... uh... what's the red fruit called? Apple? no..." — production disguised as correcting an idiot.

You note: performance pressure is the enemy; all future production probes go through play, never spotlight.

→ Beat 7.

4the echo trap

"What's your name?" → "What's your name?" — echoed back, perfect accent, zero comprehension.

You do: smile, answer the "question" ("I'm Idan!") and try one more with the puppet answering first as a model.

The puppet gets THE look. (Twelve. Obviously.)

80:32–0:42

The fork — convert or keep climbing

Frontier found → conversion to teaching · Frontier not found → A4 generative

If the frontier is mapped (the near-zero and middle kids): session one converts into a miniature of the skeleton's blocks 3–5 — teach 5–7 workhorse words from her own treasure hunt plus one wow-word, TPR-style, then a quick movement game with them. These are the first real tracker entries, born on her territory.

If she's still climbing (the stealth-fluent kid): A4 — her game's screenshot, "what's happening here?", a real 4–6 turn mini-conversation. Clean pass: 3+ multi-word utterances she composed herself.

⚠any beat

Global interrupts — cut in at any beat, override everything

Siren. Session pauses calmly, shelter together. Shelter time is neighbor time; English optional. Her sense of safety outranks the lesson, always — and how you behave in the stairwell IS the relationship being built.
Tears / frustration. Switch activity immediately, return to a past win, never end on the hard thing. The beat you were in is abandoned without ceremony and without comment.
Parent hovering, correcting, answering for her. Give the parent a job: "תרשמי כל מילה באנגלית שאת שומעת ממנה" — "write down every English word you hear her say." The hoverer becomes your scribe; the kid gets an audience instead of a judge; you get a second inventory list.
Sibling wants in (live risk at the sisters'). Don't eject them — assign a role ("you're the judge of who jumps better"), finish the beat, and note it: the together-vs-separate decision just got real data.
You blank on the plan. Default move, always ready: "teach me something." It's A2, it's rapport, and it buys 5 minutes while the shape comes back to you.

Back to Claude — the debrief package

This is the moment the whole system was built for: simulation meets reality, and v1 becomes v1.1. Bring:

  • The filled scoring sheet — including the circled beat grades, so we can diff reality against this simulation branch by branch.
  • Timing truth — which beats ran long, which evaporated. (My prediction to falsify: the treasure hunt runs long on every kid, and A3 runs short.)
  • Grid-vs-kid moments — every place you abandoned the plan, and whether you're glad you did.
  • The 10-minute claim — did the scoring sheet actually take ≤10 minutes from memory? If not, the sheet is over-specified and we cut it.
  • One thing no branch predicted — there will be at least one. That's the most valuable line in the whole debrief.
What we do with itRecalibrate pass bars if they mis-sorted her · adjust beat timings to observed reality · add the unpredicted branch to this simulation · seed session two from the "first teaching chunk" line · and if the It-Takes-Two probe surprised us in either direction, the student note gets rewritten before the parents' next report can.
