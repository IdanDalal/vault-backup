# Tutoring — Universal Baseline (first contact, any student)

Adaptive-staircase instrument for session one with **any** student — replaces per-student baseline designs. It assumes nothing about level because it finds the level: start where success is guaranteed, climb while she succeeds, and the frontier where success stops **is** the baseline. Companion to [[tutoring-session-skeleton]], which takes over from session two.

## Ground rules

1. **Reports load the bag, never the rung.** Parent/child level reports are noise — discard. Exposure observations ("his games are in English") are prep signal — they choose which props and vocabulary domains to bring. Everyone starts at rung zero regardless.
2. **Exposure ≠ comprehension.** Evidence: Idi's neighbor (40+, music-editing job with some English) played *It Takes Two* start to finish with his son and asked afterwards whether there were more levels — the game's emphatically resolved ending never reached him. Years of exposure, zero meaning absorbed. Never infer comprehension from environment; probe it directly (see the It-Takes-Two probe below).
3. **Disguise is mandatory — and is the connection engine.** Every rung is wrapped in play, and each probe is a chance to anchor English to something real: the room, her body, his games, the fridge, YouTube. Scoring happens in your head; the sheet gets filled in after she goes home.
4. **Success metric unchanged from [[tutoring-a-first-session]]:** she wants a session two. The ladder always yields to the kid — a rung skipped because she was having too much fun elsewhere is a good outcome, and the ladder finishes next time.
5. **Never end a track on a miss.** Back off to something she owns, land the win, then switch tracks.

## The ladder — two tracks, run both

Oral skill and literacy are orthogonal in kids (verbally strong + letter-blind exists, and the reverse). Run Track B even if Track A stalls early, and vice versa.

### Track A — Oral

| Rung | Probes | Game disguise | Clean pass |
|------|--------|---------------|------------|
| **A0 — Loanwords** | English already hiding in her Hebrew: pizza, telephone, OK, bye, internet, chips, + her domain's words | The treasure hunt (from the A plan): "you already speak English and nobody told you" | No bar — this rung exists for rapport and the revelation. ~8 min. |
| **A1 — Receptive** | 8 commands/selections: "jump", "touch something red", "give me the pencil" | Simon Says / "the floor is lava" variants — pure TPR, her body answers | ≥7 instant and correct |
| **A2 — Productive** | 8 objects/actions to name | Role reversal: "now YOU'RE the teacher — teach me the English words you know"; point-and-name race | ≥6 named unprompted |
| **A3 — Chunks & patterns** | "Hello, what's your name?", "How are you?", "I like ___", one two-step instruction | Puppet/stuffed-animal dialogue, or a "secret handshake" exchange built from the phrases | ≥4 of 5 exchanges land appropriately |
| **A4 — Generative** | Describe a busy picture; hold a 4–6 turn mini-conversation about her own territory; retell something tiny | "What's happening here?" with a screenshot from HER game/show; gossip about characters she loves | 3+ multi-word utterances she composed herself (not formulas) |

### Track B — Literacy

| Rung | Probes | Game disguise | Clean pass |
|------|--------|---------------|------------|
| **B1 — Letters** | 6 letters, including the ones in her own name | Letter hunt on packaging/keyboard/logos she brought | ≥5 recognized |
| **B2 — Words** | 3 CVC words (cat, dog, sun) | "Crack the code" — decoding as spy work | 2 of 3 decoded |
| **B3 — Sentence** | One short sentence, ideally about her ("Dan likes Minecraft") | The sentence is a "secret message" about her, planted somewhere in the room | Read with meaning (she reacts to the content) |

### The It-Takes-Two probe (sidecar, whenever her media territory comes up)

For any student whose exposure story includes English media/games: first, in Hebrew, ask her to tell you the *story* of her own game or show — what's happening, who wants what. Then show a moment from it (screenshot with text, or a short clip cued offline) and ask what it says / what he said. This separates the three cases that "he plays in English all day" collapses together: absorbed meaning, absorbed sounds without meaning, or absorbed nothing. It also happens to be the most connective moment of the session — you're taking her world seriously.

## Climb mechanics

- **Clean pass** → next rung. **Effortful pass** (long hesitation, half-right) → one more probe at the same rung, then decide. **Two misses** → this is the frontier; back off one rung, land a win, switch track.
- Rungs above A0 take ~5 minutes each. A near-zero student exits the whole ladder in ~15–20 minutes; a strong one spends the session climbing, and with a strong student the climb *is* engaging play — A4 done well is just conversation.
- **Conversion rule:** the moment the ladder is done and time remains, session one converts into teaching — a miniature of the skeleton's blocks 3–5, using 5–7 workhorse words that *emerged from her own treasure hunt* plus one wow-word. First tracker entries, born from her own territory.
- **Spillover rule:** if the frontier is still unfound at wrap-up time (strong student), stop climbing, end on a win. The remaining rungs open session two. A baseline that takes a session and a half is more accurate than one rushed into 45 minutes.
- **Re-run at ~3 months** = the retention audit. Same instrument, same rungs — the delta is the LG/H story.

## The bag (prep, per student)

- **Generic core:** drawing supplies, small props for TPR, letter cards, the "secret message" sentence strip, phone with material cued **offline**.
- **Domain load** (this is where exposure reports finally pay): her-territory screenshots/clips, hobby objects, printed logos from his games. For the [[tutoring-cousin-son-9|cousin's son]]: his actual games' vocabulary. For the [[tutoring-sisters-9-12|sisters]]: two separate domain loads — 9 and 12 live in different worlds.

## After-session scoring sheet (fill after she leaves — becomes tracker entry #1)

```
Student:            Date:            Session length:
Track A — highest clean rung:        frontier rung + what broke:
Track B — highest clean rung:        frontier rung + what broke:
It-Takes-Two probe result:  meaning / sounds-only / nothing / not run
Inventory — words she OWNS (list them):
Domains that lit her up:
Wow-word candidates from her territory:
Surprises (either direction):
First teaching chunk for session two (5–7 words + 1 wow):
Parent note #1 sent? (lead with the discovery: "she already owns N English words")
```

Parent note #1 is special: it leads with what the child *has*, never what she lacks — "today I discovered A already owns 14 English words" is the strongest possible opening for the can-do ledger.

## Risks

Inherit the entire playbook from [[tutoring-a-first-session#Risk analysis — what might go wrong]] — shy/silent, doesn't-want-this, attention collapse, frustration, sirens, boundaries. One addition specific to this instrument: **a strong student may sense she's being assessed** (older kids especially — the 12-year-old). Counter: the higher rungs are the most conversation-like, so lean harder into genuine interest in her territory; the moment it feels like a quiz, drop the ladder and just talk — A4 data arrives on its own.
