# Lesson One — Field Guide

> [!important] The only metric
> **Session one succeeds if the kid wants a session two.** Laughing beats covering material. Confidence before grades. ~40 minutes, never 60 — end while it's still fun.

## The bag

- [ ] NEW notebook — given away in minute one
- [ ] Pencils + erasers
- [ ] ABC stickers
- [ ] Word card in pocket (never on the table)
- [ ] Phone: anything you'll play cued OFFLINE — otherwise phone stays away

## Framing (read twice before knocking)

- We're **talking AND learning English** — never "here TO learn English."
- Claude is backstage, always. The kid never talks to a chatbot. No screens between you.
- Parent in natural earshot, door open. Brief the parent: conversational by design; progress looks like confidence first.
- Script the **first 5 minutes only**. The rest has a shape, no script.

## The shape

### 1 · Minute one (at the door)

"Hello!" — the lesson has already started. Names, smiles, sit down. No syllabus speech.
**Give the notebook:** *"This is yours. You keep it."* They own something from minute one — page one of a second brain they'll build for life. Stickers can personalize the cover, their choice.

### 2 · Treasure hunt (5–10 min)

Ask in Hebrew: *"מה את/ה הכי אוהב/ת לעשות?"* / *"מה היית עושה עכשיו אם לא הייתי פה?"*
Whatever they answer is the hunting ground — dig out the English **already inside it**: loanwords, game words, song words, YouTube words. Fallback: hunt the room (pizza, telephone, computer, OK, bye).
The revelation lands by itself: ==you already speak some English.== Never state it as a fact — let them prove it (the fact is a discovery, never a statement).

### 3 · Words + notebook (10 min)

- **5–7 workhorse words** from *their* world, used many times each. Mastery before volume.
- **+ 1 wow-word** as dessert — kids love owning an expert word; the workhorses are what compound.
- **Writing marks the click:** talk continuously; the notebook opens only when a word LANDS. Writing crowns each small win — it never interrupts. At level zero they write/draw words; the moment they can, it's their OWN sentence about their OWN thing, never a copied list.

### 4 · Game (5–8 min)

Movement-based: you say a word, they point / touch / act it. Bodies, not paper.

### 5 · End on a win (2 min)

They say **"Bye! See you!"** — then tell them (Hebrew is fine) ONE thing they did great today.

## Rhythm rules

- ~10 min per activity max. Rotate before attention collapses.
- A dead activity gets dropped without ceremony.
- Return to a past win whenever anything wobbles. Never end on the hard thing.

## The interior trapdoor (dessert — optional, not lesson one for beginners)

> [!tip] The move
> **"You think you know this word... let's look inside it!"**
> Best words: attention · time · observer · measurement · space · reality · energy · random · information · nothing. Open one ONLY when their moment brushes it — lag → *time*, "watch me!" → *attention*, loot drop → *random*. Sometimes the inside is EMPTY — humanity never settled it — and that's the treasure: the kid stands at the same open door as every genius. With the girls this runs in Hebrew; the concept is the lesson, English keeps the workhorses.

Dynamics/Moves are for the English-saturated kid (and wow-word dessert); with near-zero beginners the real ammo is workhorses from their world. The moment picks the shot — the list is only what you carry.

## Dialogue moves (from 50 years of P4C — see [[P4C-research]])

- **The four openers** — the whole question toolkit: *Why do you think that?* · *Can you say more?* · *What do you mean by ___?* · *Can you give an example?*
- **Closed grammar, open concept:** "Is it the same game after the update?" — answerable with כן/לא and a pointed finger, philosophically bottomless. The low-English kid's door into deep water.
- **Imaginary disagreer** (replaces the missing classroom at 1-on-1): *"What would someone who disagrees with you say?"*
- **Echo their words back verbatim** — never upgrade a kid's idea into adult-speak.
- **The enemy reflex is the quiz:** ask → answer → "right!" kills the dialogue. The third move extends, never grades.
- One question at a time. Your own opinion stays home.

## Risk playbook

| If... | Then... |
| --- | --- |
| Shy / silent | Normal "silent period" — never force speech. Pointing, drawing, nodding, Hebrew answers all count; you reply in simple English. |
| Doesn't want this (parent arranged it) | Only metric is wanting session two. Play more, cover less. |
| Level surprises you (lower/higher) | Treasure hunt IS the diagnostic. Fallback: colors + room objects. Stretch: they teach YOU a word. |
| Attention collapses | Rotate now. Drop the activity, no ceremony. |
| Frustration / tears | Switch immediately → return to a past win → never end on the hard thing. |
| Ice-breaker falls flat | It's decoration, the structure doesn't need it. Pivot: "what music/games do you like?" — English lives in the answer. |
| Sirens mid-session | Pause calmly, go down together. Shelter time is neighbor time; English optional. Their sense of safety outranks everything. |
| Your nerves | First 5 minutes are scripted. That's all you need. The shape carries the rest. |
