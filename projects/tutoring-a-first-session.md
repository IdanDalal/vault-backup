# First English Session — A (9)

Free English tutoring for the downstairs neighbors' daughter, arranged via Mom. Level: near zero (but see treasure hunt — she owns more English than anyone thinks). Already familiar with me from the building's bomb-shelter gatherings; I've seen her drawing there.

Philosophy sources: the three transcripts in `inbox/TUTORING/` — effortful practice & cognitive load (Veritasium), communion-before-community / dive right in (Bateson warm data), teacher as personal trainer. Framing: we're sitting here talking **and** learning English (never "to learn English").

## Session plan (~40 min, not 60 — end while it's still fun)

1. **Hello (5 min).** "Hello, A!" at the door — the lesson has already started. Names, smiles, sit down. No syllabus speech; role/goals/plans come later or next time.
2. **The A moment (5 min).** "You have a famous name. She sings in English — listen." Play the chorus of *Hello*. Teach: **hello, it's, me**. She sings it back. She's sung English within 10 minutes of starting.
3. **Interest question → treasure hunt (10 min).** Ask (in Hebrew): "What do you like doing most?" / "What would you be doing right now if I wasn't here?" Whatever she answers becomes the hunting ground for English she already owns. Likely answer: drawing/coloring (seen in the shelter — come prepared with that vocab). Generic fallback: hunt the room — pizza, telephone, computer, OK, bye. The revelation lands by itself: *you already speak some English*.
4. **The notebook (10 min).** She writes/draws today's words — the ones that emerged, including the song words. The notebook records what happened; it doesn't dictate what will.
5. **Game with today's words (5–8 min).** Movement-based: I say a word, she points/touches/acts it.
6. **End on a win (2 min).** She says "Bye! See you!" — and I tell her (Hebrew fine) one thing she did great.

**Vocabulary load:** 5–7 high-frequency workhorse words used many times (draw, color, paper, red, blue, I like…) plus **one wow-word** per session as dessert ("palette") — kids love owning an expert word, but the workhorses are what compound. Mastery before volume.

**Prep:** *Hello* chorus cued offline on phone (no buffering/ads); first-page words written out big and clear; drawing supplies on hand.

## A song shortlist (lyrics verified line-by-line, June 2026)

| Song | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Hello** (25) | The ice-breaker: opens "Hello, it's me"; verse 2 opens "Hello, how are you?" — lesson-one dialogue. Slow, crystal enunciation. |
| Make You Feel My Love (19) | Slowest, clearest singing in the catalog; concrete nature words (rain, stars, sea, wind). |
| Easy on Me (30) | Short repeated chorus; "go easy on me" is a usable phrase; the one her friends will recognize. |
| Lovesong (21, Cure cover) | Lowest-vocab A song; chorus is a fill-in-the-blank adjective drill ("you make me feel like I am *young/fun/free* again"). |
| Skyfall (2012) | Simplest grammar: "Where you go, I go / What you see, I see" — pronoun+verb pattern; Bond drama keeps attention. |

**Avoid despite seeming safe:** Rolling in the Deep & Hometown Glory (profanity), Daydreamer (suggestive line on the debut), Hold On (depression wording a child can parse), everything on 30 except Easy on Me.

## Risk analysis — what might go wrong

- **Shy/silent** → normal "silent period"; never force speech. She can point, draw, nod, answer in Hebrew while I reply in simple English. (Risk reduced: we already know each other from the shelter.)
- **Doesn't want this** (Mom arranged it) → the only success metric for session one is *she wants a session two*. Laughing beats covering material.
- **Level surprises me** (lower or higher) → the treasure hunt is the diagnostic; fallback = colors via room objects, stretch = she teaches *me* a word.
- **Attention collapses** → ~10 min per activity max; plan already rotates every 5–10 min; drop a dead activity without ceremony.
- **Frustration/tears** → switch activity immediately, return to a past win, never end on the hard thing.
- **A-the-singer falls flat** → it's an ice-breaker, the structure doesn't depend on it; pivot to "what music do you like?" — it'll have English in it.
- **Sirens mid-session** (war with Iran, building shelters together) → session pauses calmly, go down together; shelter time is just neighbor time, English optional. Her sense of safety outranks the lesson, always.
- **Setting/boundaries** → parent in natural earshot, door open; brief Mom that it's conversational by design — progress looks like confidence before it looks like grades.
- **My nerves** (big first) → script only the first 5 minutes (hello + song); the rest has a shape, no script. We're talking **and** learning English.
