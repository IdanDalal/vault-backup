# I4 core: signal vs noise

## 1. Thesis (3 sentences)
Cobb's unspent love for Mal becomes the Mal projection, which acts as a therapist: she runs Toohey's soul-breaking method in reverse, closing each of Cobb's escape routes until the only exit is "I have to let you go." Cobb is an omnipotent dreamer (limbo) who invented constraints (murder charge, mission, hidden faces) as avoidance, so his release makes the reality question irrelevant. Nolan runs the same inception on the viewer (the cut, the broken totem logic, the 2:28 match), planting "state of peace > state of matter", and the video essay (I4) repeats the move on its own audience without ever saying so.

## 3. Load-bearing chain
- A. Claim 1 (Mal = grief with agency). Without it Mal is an antagonist and the surgery reading has no subject.
- B. Claim 3 (ordered escalation). The only film-side evidence that Mal is purposeful. It is also where the text errors cluster (section 5), so it is the weakest joint.
- C. Claim 4 ("I have to let you go" as end state). Holds; this is on screen.
- D. Claim 11 (Cobb is an omnipotent limbo dreamer). Level III, the convenience cascade, the "invented constraints" and the infinity-of-selves ending all need it. The counterargument registry dismisses "All Dream Trope" as low validity while the theory depends on an all-dream premise. Unresolved.
- E. Claim 18 plus 25 (Nolan deliberately incepts the viewer; the right answer is to stop asking). Layer 3 and the whole I4 essay stand on it. Claim 18 is real and does most of the work; 21 and 22 are ornaments.
- Toohey (2) is the operating metaphor; the theory survives without it (plain "therapist Mal"), the originality does not.
- Dispensable: ring, hotel window, 2:28, wobble, convenience cascade, the six philosophy quotes. Each is decoration on A through E.

Structural risk: the Q-system rewards readings that feel deeper (Q1) and treats inconsistency as proof of design (I3 pattern, "convenience IS the evidence"). That makes the theory unfalsifiable by construction. Every item in the compendium scores HIGH; the scoring filters nothing.

## 5. Likely factual errors about the film
1. Hotel layer numbering and event. Canon arc map calls the hotel "Dream Layer 1" and says Mal "sabotages the mission by appearing to Fischer" there. The hotel is layer 2 (the rain city is layer 1), and Mal does not appear to Fischer in the hotel. Her mission interference is the freight train in layer 1 and the shot in layer 3. High confidence. This breaks Phase 2 of the funnel.
2. "Paris café attack." The café dream ends in the café exploding and projections turning; Mal stabs Ariadne in a later Paris training dream. Medium.
3. "Desperate dreams of someone who wants to feel important" is not a line I know from the film. Mal's actual limbo argument is about being "chased around the globe by anonymous corporations and police forces, the way the projections persecute the dreamer". Treat as paraphrase presented as a quote. Medium-high.
4. Ariadne "no follow-up, no final exchange" after "I have to let you go": she still speaks ("Don't lose yourself", find Saito) before her kick, and she exchanges a look with Cobb on the plane. High.
5. "It never wobbled before": the top falls in the earlier gun scene, so it has wobbled on screen. Medium-high.
6. 2:28: 148 min is the full runtime including credits, so the story ends near 2h20m and the credits sit inside the 2:28. That contradicts "song ends, we wake up (credits)". The 2:28 song length is itself disputed (common recordings run ~2:19 to 2:21), and I recall Zimmer calling the match a coincidence. Medium on both.
7. Totem rules: in the film, a totem works because only its owner knows its weight/feel, so it feels wrong in someone else's dream. "Abnormal in reality, normal in dreams" is the author's construction. Eames's misspelled-chip rule is not in the film. High on the chip, medium on the framing.
8. Hotel window: Mal says she arranged the facing room herself; the film shows two rooms across a gap, so "come back in" is not impossible. The mirrored rooms are real; the paradox is overstated. Medium.
9. Gun scene: canon says he is "DEVASTATED when top falls" (wants the dream); arc map says he is ready to die "if it keeps spinning" (wants reality). The two readings contradict each other; the on-screen reaction reads as subdued, not devastated. Low-medium.
10. Saito buys the airline "in days" vs "in hours": internal inconsistency; on screen it is one phone call. Low stakes.
11. Nolan "the character doesn't care" dated 2023: I recall it from his 2015 Princeton commencement speech. Medium; verify before using.
12. "Academic consensus... CONFIRMED" with no citation: unsupported.
Accurate as far as I can check: "Come back to reality" (Miles), "She'll be back", "You remind me of someone", "I have to let you go", the cut quote (Nolan, Wired, 2010), two sets of child actors.

## 6. Gut: most original idea
Mal as a therapist running Toohey's soul-breaking method in reverse, and the funnel test that goes with it: Mal blocks Cobb's escape routes, not his goal. That test ("what exit does this appearance close?") is a real, falsifiable reading rule. The viewer-inception half and "does the top matter" are widely argued already; this part is new. Its weak spot is Phase 2, which rests on a scene that does not exist as described.
