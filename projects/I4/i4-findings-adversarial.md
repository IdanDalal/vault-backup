## 4. Falsification criteria

None stated for the core theory in these three files. The nearest lines are local and conditional:

- Attack 1: "Confirmation bias would produce SCATTERED evidence." This implies scattered evidence would count against the theory, but no measure of scatter is given.
- Attack 8: "If Mal's actions vaguely resemble Toohey's, the attack has merit." and "The mapping could fail at any method."
- Attack 2: "Attack valid for isolated details."

Structural immunizers that work against falsifiability:

- Q1: "If the patterns are imaginary, they still enhance viewing. Value exists regardless of intent."
- Attack 5: "The viewer who declares reality based on wobble has FAILED the test."
- Attack 10: the therapeutic model fits both critical and supportive figments.

Outside these files, only `canon/critiques/critique-01.md` (a NotebookLM transcript) uses falsifiability, to rank single clues ("could someone actually prove this wrong?"); it names no result that would sink the theory. A candidate the author could adopt: Nolan's own Wired rule ("there can't be anything in the film that tells you one way or another") makes any decisive in-film clue, dream or real, a counterexample to the method he relies on.
