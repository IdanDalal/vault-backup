# Eero Alvar - Agent Harness for Learning Things

## What it says

- [0:20] Goal: maximum understanding built per unit of mental effort, where the effort goes into direct contact with the material.
- [0:50] "The biggest risk in learning with AI is not hallucinations or errors by the LLM. It is accidentally skipping the struggle part and creating sort of an illusion of learning."
- [1:20] Understanding a thing and being able to use it are separate. Recall runs on cues, and a new idea starts with a narrow set of cues.
- [2:30] Two processes: teaching installs understanding; practice widens and strengthens the cues.
- [3:00] "Optimal" only exists relative to what the learner already understands, so the agent needs a detailed picture of how the learner thinks. A right or wrong answer does not give that.
- [3:40] **Thinking fields**: next to almost every interaction, a box where the learner dumps raw, messy thinking. That stream is the agent's data.
- [4:10] Teaching in three phases. Probe: quick multiple-choice questions that find the edge of what you know. Plan: the shortest dependency chain from what you know to the goal. Teach: one reasoning step at a time, each one landing as "obviously true."
- [5:00] Each step: you predict, the agent explains, the agent checks. Two questions per step.
- [6:00] Practice: a stream of problems that adapts by how many ideas a problem combines and how familiar its cues are. Target: "recall that succeeds with effort." Hints are minimal nudges.
- [8:00] Memory between sessions: propositions and reasoning chains, drawn as a graph whose arrows mean "this leads to that."
- [8:50] "In every other harness, the human is the operator... here you are the system being acted on."
- [12:00] A confidence slider per answer. [17:00] Walls of text are bad here because they starve the agent of data about the learner.
- The app: free, open source, desktop, "you just plug your own keys in."
