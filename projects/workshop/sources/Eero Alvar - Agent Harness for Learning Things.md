# Eero Alvar - Agent Harness for Learning Things

jep laid out the highlights. The space under each one is Idi's, and jep leaves it alone. Tick `processed` when the source is spent.

## Highlights

### 1. [0:50] The biggest risk

> "I think the biggest risk in learning with AI is not hallucinations or errors by the LLM. It is accidentally skipping the struggle part and creating sort of an illusion of learning."



### 2. [1:10] Where the effort goes

> "What we can do is allocate our mental effort better so that a maximal share of it goes into direct interaction with the material, the stuff that most contributes to learning, and a minimal share to everything else."



### 3. [2:10] Narrow cues

> "A newly learned idea only really comes reliably to mind in the context that it was taught in."



### 4. [3:05] No optimal lesson in the abstract

> "There's no optimal lesson or optimal practice problem in the abstract."



### 5. [3:50] Same wrong answer

> "Two people can have the same wrong answer for very different reasons."



### 6. [4:50] Obviously true

> "The goal level of understanding is something feeling obviously true."



### 7. [6:40] Effortful recall

> "The goal is recall that succeeds with effort."



### 8. [9:00] Who is the system

> "In every other harness, the human is the operator, the one driving the agent. But, here you are the system being acted on."



## jep's notes

- The mechanism: thinking fields (the learner dumps raw thinking next to every answer; that stream is the agent's data); teaching as probe, plan, teach; each step as predict, explain, check; practice problems that adapt by how many ideas they combine and how familiar the cues are; memory as propositions and reasoning chains drawn as a graph.
- Measured: nothing. The struggle claim matches the "desirable difficulties" research line (Robert Bjork), unchecked here.
