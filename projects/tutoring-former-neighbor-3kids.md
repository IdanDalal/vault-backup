## Why E is not a UAB student

[[tutoring-universal-baseline]] is an instrument for finding an unknown floor in a child who may have none. E has a floor and knows roughly where it is; what she lacks is a **ceiling under pressure** — the specific gap between "can converse" and "can hold a room for twenty minutes in a second language." Different diagnosis, different instrument:

- The baseline for her is a **performance sample**, not a staircase: she talks about her own work for 3–5 minutes and Idi listens for what actually breaks — hesitation points, circumlocution, register, filler, recovery after a stumble, stress and rhythm at sentence level.
- Her self-report is **usable evidence**, unlike a parent's report about a child — adults have calibrated self-models and can name the situations that scare them. Ask which presentation, to whom, in what room.
- Motivation is intrinsic and specific, which removes the entire disguise apparatus. No games, no props, no treasure hunt.
- The adult failure mode is the mirror image of a child's: not shame at not knowing, but **shame at sounding less intelligent in English than she is in Hebrew.** Everything in her plan should be built around protecting that.
- Free-tutoring framing needs a deliberate answer here: an adult peer receiving free lessons carries a different social weight than a child does, and she may prefer some form of reciprocity. Decide the framing before the first session, not during it.

## Open questions for E (collect before the plan is written)

- Presentations to whom — colleagues, clients, an international audience? Live or on video calls?
- What's her actual deadline, if any? A real upcoming presentation is the best possible curriculum.
- Which is worse for her right now: finding words, being understood, or understanding fast speech from others?
- Frequency and duration she can genuinely sustain.
