# P4C Research — Philosophy for Children & Equivalents

*Researched 2026-07-06 on Idi's word, days before lesson one. Web research by a jep sub-agent; sources inline; unverified items flagged. The "How this lands in jep" section at the end is the bridge to [[lesson-one-field-guide]] and the Interior 10.*

## 1. What P4C actually is

**Origin & core idea.** Matthew Lipman (with Ann Margaret Sharp) launched P4C in the early 1970s at the Institute for the Advancement of Philosophy for Children (IAPC), Montclair State. Bet: children can already reason; give them philosophically provocative narrative and a structured dialogue and they respond philosophically ([p4c.com history](https://p4c.com/about-p4c/history-of-p4c/), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/children/)).

**Community of inquiry (CoI).** From Peirce via Dewey: a caring-but-critical group of collaborators who raise questions and examine candidate answers together. The teacher's job is *reasonableness* — participants willing "to be reasoned with" and to change their minds ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/children/), [Philosophy Foundation P4C page](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/p4c)).

**Canonical session structure** (SAPERE-style): stimulus (story/video/object) → children generate their own philosophical questions → group chooses one (voting) → facilitated dialogue building on each other's ideas → review/last thoughts ([Structural Learning guide](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/p4c), [TeachingTimes](https://www.teachingtimes.com/knowledge-banks/philosophy-for-children/)).

**Facilitator role.** Not knowledge-giver; questions to push for reasons, connect concepts, deepen/widen inquiry. Facilitators may "share puzzlement with their students" — they need not have answers ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/children/), [Permanent Education P4C Plus](https://permanenteducation.org/p4c-plus/)). Lipman's "4 Cs": critical, creative, caring, collaborative thinking ([Philosophy Foundation](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/p4c)).

## 2. Evidence — honestly mixed

**EEF trial 1 (Gorard et al., 2015).** RCT, 48 schools, 3,159 pupils. Reported ~2 months extra progress; effect sizes 0.12 reading, 0.10 maths, 0.07 cognitive; bigger for free-school-meals pupils ([EEF project page](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/philosophy-for-children), [Gorard 2017 paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1467-9752.12227)). **Caveats:** publicly criticized for no significance testing, deviation from the original analysis plan, data-driven change-score analysis and favorable subgroup selection; the CAT (cognitive ability) measure showed **no effect** ([Schools Week: EEF defends report](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/eef-defends-philosophy-report/), [meta-analysis noting null CAT result](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/atpp/atpp-1160.pdf)).

**EEF trial 2 (NFER, 2021) — the big one.** 198 schools (75 intervention, 123 control), KS2 national tests as outcome, pre-registered. Result: **no additional progress** in reading or maths; Hedges g 0.01–0.05; EEF's highest security rating (5/5 padlocks); EEF dropped further trials ([EEF effectiveness trial page](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/philosophy-for-children-effectiveness-trial), [ripe-tomato analysis](https://ripe-tomato.org/2021/03/09/philosophy-for-children-p4c-trial-2/) — "well-designed negative trial… has not missed a worthwhile small effect").

**Meta-analyses.** A 2025 three-level meta-analysis (33 studies, 4,568 participants) finds overall g = 0.59 on cognitive outcomes, robust for **reasoning, critical thinking, creativity** — but smaller samples report larger effects (classic small-study bias) ([Journal of Intelligence / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12565533/), [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/13/10/130)). An earlier meta-analysis: d = 0.58 overall, reasoning d = 1.06 ([Analytic Teaching & Philosophical Praxis](https://journal.viterbo.edu/index.php/atpp/article/view/1160)).

**Honest reading for this project:** P4C reliably moves *proximal* skills — reasoning, argument quality, talk — in small studies; it does **not** move standardized reading/maths attainment at scale. Since the goal here is dialogue and conceptual depth (never test scores), the negative EEF result is not damning for this use case — but never claim "proven to boost English."

## 3. Current state (2024–2026) & living equivalents

- **SAPERE (UK)** — Lipman-lineage charity, ran both EEF trials. Rebuilt its training pathway: Level 1 launched 2024, Level 2 2025, Level 3 due 2026; "Extend P4C" strands for SEND and literacy ([sapere.org.uk pathway](https://www.sapere.org.uk/our-courses/p4c-pathway/)).
- **PLATO (US)** — active national organization; free **Philosophy Toolkit** (searchable lesson plans by topic/age), resource library, webinars, courses with mentorship ([plato-philosophy.org](https://plato-philosophy.org/), [Toolkit](https://plato-philosophy.org/philosophy-toolkit/)). Probably the best free question/lesson bank for a lone tutor.
- **The Philosophy Foundation (UK, Peter Worley)** — PhiE method; free online **enquiries database tagged by age** (e.g. [ages 5–7 list](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/enquiries/age:37), [Knowledge enquiry with the black/red card certainty game](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/enquiries/view/knowledge)); books: *The If Machine*, *The Philosophy Shop*, *Once Upon an If* ([parents' guide](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/blog/philosophy-club-a-parents-guide)).
- **p4c.com** — subscription resource cooperative ([p4c.com](https://p4c.com/homepage-2/)).
- **Brila (Montreal, Natalie Fletcher)** — "Philocreation": philosophy fused with creative making ("philozines"); camps and workshops, now with a nature campus ([brila.org](https://www.brila.org/home), [Concordia news 2024](https://www.concordia.ca/cunews/offices/advancement/2024/01/25/concordia-alumna-opens-new-space-in-nature-for-curious-minds.html)).
- **Dialogic Teaching (Robin Alexander)** — the non-philosophy cousin; its EEF trial (2014–17, Year 5) was one of only ~17 of 190 EEF trials with clearly positive results ([robinalexander.org.uk](https://robinalexander.org.uk/dialogic-teaching/), [EEF trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/dialogic-teaching), [Alexander's summary PDF](https://coleridgeprimary.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Dialogc-teaching-in-brief-170622.pdf)). Key concept: escape the IRE pattern (initiate–respond–evaluate) so pupils reason, argue, explain rather than answer-and-stop.
- **Scandinavia/Denmark** — "Filosofi i Skolen" (FiSK), University of Southern Denmark research collaboration ([Philosophy Foundation page](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/filosofi-i-skolen)). *Could not verify current details of Norwegian wonder-based practitioners (e.g., Finn Thorbjørn Hansen's "wonder labs") — flagged as unverified.*
- **ICPIC** — the international umbrella, still active ([icpic.org](https://www.icpic.org/about-us/)).
- **Israel specifically:** P4wC is *not* formally taught in Israeli universities/colleges; a University of Haifa MA seminar runs an annual mixed Jewish/Muslim/Christian/Druze/Circassian P4wC group; ICPIC's 2007 conference was at Hebrew University ([Madan article](https://madan.org.il/en/news/philosophy-children-neglected-israels-education)). Near-empty local territory.

## 4. The 1:1 adaptation problem

**The tension is real.** P4C's engine is *peer* disagreement and idea-diversity; Worley recommends "no less than 8 children" for a philosophy club precisely for diversity of ideas ([parents' guide](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/blog/philosophy-club-a-parents-guide)). Question-voting, peer response chains, and community norms all evaporate at n=1.

**But a parallel 1:1 tradition exists:**
- **Gareth Matthews, *Philosophy and the Young Child* (Harvard UP, 1980)** — built from individual conversations with children; documented sustained philosophical thinking (a 4-year-old on why things "don't really get smaller" from a plane; a 5-year-old on measuring time) and argued Piaget systematically underestimated children ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/children/), [SHCY on *The Child's Philosopher*](https://www.shcy.org/features/books/gareth-b-matthews-the-childs-philosopher/)).
- **Jana Mohr Lone, *The Philosophical Child* (2012)** — explicit parent-to-one-child guidance on "gently approaching philosophical questions with children of all ages"; director, UW Center for Philosophy for Children ([UW page](https://phil.washington.edu/research/books/philosophical-child)).
- **Scott Hershovitz, *Nasty, Brutish, and Short* (2022)** — dialogues with his own two sons; claims kids ~3–7 spontaneously raise philosophical questions and adults decide whether to nurture it ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/05/12/1096811309/nasty-brutish-and-short-contends-that-all-parents-are-raising-little-philosopher)).

**What transfers to 1:1:** stimulus-first entry; contestable-concept questions; the opening-up move set (§5); tutor neutrality; letting the child's puzzlement steer. **What breaks and its replacement:** missing peers → Worley's **"imaginary disagreer"** ("what would someone who disagrees with you say?") makes the child generate the opposing voice themselves ([Philosophy Now on Worley](https://philosophynow.org/issues/84/Peter_Worley)); the tutor can also play devil's advocate *in character* (a puppet, a fictional person) to avoid becoming the authority who "knows the answer." **Flag: no controlled research on 1:1 philosophical dialogue efficacy exists — the 1:1 literature is practitioner/anecdotal (Matthews, Lone, Hershovitz), not trial-based.**

## 5. Practical harvest — moves, questions, ages, pitfalls

**Worley's move set** (the most concrete facilitation tech in the field; sources: [Philosophers' Magazine, "How To Philosophise with Children"](https://philosophersmag.com/essays/50-how-to-philosophise-with-children), [TPF blog on anchoring](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/blog/new-facilitation-tool-self-anchoring), [open/closed questions paper](https://jps.bham.ac.uk/articles/44/files/submission/proof/44-1-85-1-10-20200211.pdf)):

- **Grammatically closed, conceptually open questions**: "Is it the same pen?" (yes/no surface, identity underneath). The closed form lowers the language bar — a big deal for near-zero-English kids — while the concept stays contestable.
- **The four openers**: *Why do you think that?* (justify) / *Can you say more?* (elicit) / *What do you mean by X?* (clarify) / *Can you give an example?* (exemplify).
- **Iffing & anchoring**: restate the child's claim hypothetically ("*if* the clock stopped, would time stop?") and anchor it back to the main question.
- **Echo, don't paraphrase**: repeat the child's words verbatim rather than interpreting/upgrading them ([parents' guide](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/blog/philosophy-club-a-parents-guide)).
- **Respond minimally**; never state your own view.
- **Test implications**: "What does that tell us about...?"

**On the deep-common words:** PLATO's [Toolkit](https://plato-philosophy.org/philosophy-toolkit/) and TPF's [enquiries database](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/enquiries/view/knowledge) are searchable by concept; TPF's "Knowledge" enquiry (guess which face-down card is black; "do you *know* or *believe*?") is a ready-made template for measurement/observer-type words. *Specific published question sets for "attention," "measurement," "observer" as such: not found — composing these is on us, using the closed-form/open-concept pattern (e.g. "Can you pay attention to two things at once?", "If nobody looked at the moon, would it still be there?"). Flagged: those two examples are the sub-agent's synthesis of the method, not sourced items.*

**Age-appropriateness.** The 2025 meta-analysis found effects **consistent across grade levels** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12565533/)); SEP reports even ages 4–6 engage philosophically ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/children/)). Practical difference at 9 vs 12 is mostly stimulus and stamina, not capacity. *No rigorous 9-vs-12 breakdown found — flagged.*

**Known pitfalls** (sourced): teachers find it genuinely hard to hold a facilitative style and drift back to instructing ([JLDHE educator reflections](https://journal.aldinhe.ac.uk/index.php/jldhe/article/view/1389)); the default IRE exchange (ask–answer–evaluate) turns dialogue into a quiz — the exact failure mode Alexander's dialogic teaching targets ([Dialogic Teaching in Brief PDF](https://coleridgeprimary.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Dialogc-teaching-in-brief-170622.pdf)); Worley: "one must not confuse philosophy with a classroom procedure" — running the steps mechanically produces no inquiry ([Philosophers' Magazine](https://philosophersmag.com/essays/50-how-to-philosophise-with-children)); stating your own view, paraphrasing children's ideas into adult-speak, and stacking multiple questions are all explicitly warned against ([parents' guide](https://www.philosophy-foundation.org/blog/philosophy-club-a-parents-guide)).

## 6. Second-language angle

Small but encouraging literature: P4C-style interventions in EFL classrooms **reduced English-speaking anxiety, raised motivation, and improved language skills**; "philosophy-based language teaching" (PBLT) outperformed traditional methods on speaking, critical thinking, and motivation ([ResearchGate: P4C with EFL learners](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/321879488_Philosophy_for_Children_with_learners_of_English_as_a_Foreign_Language), [EIKI JETM: P4C in EFL integrated skills](https://journals.eikipub.com/index.php/jetm/article/view/62)). P4C + English picture-storybooks improved adolescent EFL learners' reading/thinking in quasi-experiments (same sources). **Serious caveat:** one prominent supporting study in BMC Psychology was **RETRACTED** ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-024-01648-2)), and the field is small-sample and journal-quality-mixed. Treat "P4C boosts L2 acquisition" as a *plausible mechanism* (meaningful communicative need + low-stakes talk), never as established fact. Relevant note from the 2025 meta-analysis: P4C works in everyday language, no technical jargon — inherently compatible with limited vocabulary ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12565533/)).

## Steal this / Avoid this

**Steal:**
1. Session skeleton: stimulus → first thoughts → question → dialogue → last words; keep the stimulus concrete (object, card game, short story) — never the abstract word first.
2. Grammatically-closed / conceptually-open questions (perfect for low English).
3. The four openers (why / say more / what do you mean / example) as the entire question toolkit.
4. Imaginary disagreer + iffing/anchoring — the two moves that replace the missing group at n=1.
5. Echo verbatim; never paraphrase; never give your view; one question at a time.
6. PLATO Toolkit + Philosophy Foundation enquiries database + *The If Machine* / *The Philosophy Shop* as stimulus banks; Lone's *The Philosophical Child* and Hershovitz for the 1:1 stance.
7. Games/warm-up before dialogue (Worley's club structure) — earns attention before demanding it.

**Avoid:**
1. Claiming attainment benefits — the definitive large trial found none (g ≤ 0.05).
2. IRE quiz mode: ask, get an answer, evaluate it. The third move should extend, never grade.
3. Running the procedure without live puzzlement ("procedure ≠ philosophy").
4. Abstract-word-first openings ("today: what is reality?").
5. Talking too much / drifting into instructing — the documented #1 facilitator failure.
6. Leaning on the P4C-for-ESL literature as proof (retraction, small samples).

**Could not verify:** Norwegian wonder-pedagogy specifics (Hansen); published question sets for attention/observer/measurement; any controlled study of 1:1 philosophical dialogue; rigorous age-9-vs-12 differentiation.

## How this lands in jep (bridge, 2026-07-06)

1. **The jep design was already P4C-shaped before knowing P4C existed.** Experience-first/definition-last = stimulus-first. "The fact is a discovery, never a statement" = community-of-inquiry facilitation. The build-trap guard ("procedure ≠ philosophy") = Worley's own warning. The Interior 10's "sometimes the inside is empty" = the facilitator who "shares puzzlement" and needs no answers. Convergent evolution; the tradition confirms the instincts and adds fifty years of field-tested moves.
2. **The single biggest import: grammatically-closed / conceptually-open questions.** "Is it the same game after the update?" — a near-zero-English kid can answer with כן/לא and a pointed finger, while standing inside the philosophy of identity. This solves the interior-class-at-level-zero problem in English earlier than expected.
3. **Second import: the four openers + imaginary disagreer.** Four questions are the whole toolkit; the disagreer replaces the missing classroom at n=1. Also: echo verbatim, never paraphrase a kid's idea into adult-speak — the Generation Effect's conversational twin.
4. **The named enemy has a name: IRE.** Ask–answer–evaluate is the quiz reflex, the documented #1 drift, and the conversational form of supply-side teaching. The third move extends ("can you give an example?"), never grades ("right!").
5. **Honest claims only:** to parents, this is confidence and conversation — never "philosophically proven grades."
6. Stimulus banks to raid when composing interior-word enquiries: PLATO Toolkit, TPF enquiries database (TPF's card-game "Knowledge" enquiry is the template for *observer*/*measurement*).
