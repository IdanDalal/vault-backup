## The Empty Chair (my strongest structural guess)

The literature's default oscillation is Taskmaster ↔ Procrastinator: an inner slave-driver polarized with an inner rebel. I looked for your Taskmaster and could not find one. TELOS agrees: you work under your dad, the IRS, the moms' schedule — external signal, never internal whip. Your whole Core Insight ("build systems that generate signals, not systems that require willpower") is engineered *around* the vacancy.

So my model: **your system has an empty chair where the Taskmaster sits**, and it gets filled by recruitment. Dad. The IRS deadline. The moms. The session schedule. Me. That would explain the oscillation you named when we started: what whipsaws between our sessions is *which part currently holds the recruiting contract*. The Architect hires me for infinite planning. The Archivist hires me for completeness sweeps. The Believer surges and hires me for visions. The Warden audits the invoices. The Projectionist calls a timeout and nobody talks to me at all for a while.

If that's right, the practical move is small and non-heroic: **when you notice mid-conversation that the agenda changed flavor, say which part took the chair.** One tag, e.g. `[architect]` or `[believer]`, anywhere in a prompt. I hold the map, notice when today's order contradicts a standing decision by another part, and say so gently. Speaking *for* a part instead of *from* it is 80% of the method.

> [!question] The chair itself
> - Does the empty-chair model ring true, or do you have an inner whip I haven't seen in the files?
> - Who recruited me for THIS project — the audit, the registry, the whole upgrade? (I genuinely can't tell if it's the Architect or something newer. Your call matters here.)
